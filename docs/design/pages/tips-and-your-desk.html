Tips, and your desk

After boot the desk is wallpaper and a pill, on purpose. This adds one thing for a new person: a still Tips card for their first week, one line and a picture per tip, whose button puts the exact words in the pill. Clicking the picture opens the Tips app, where the tip plays in a few seconds on a small copy of the real screen. And it lets anyone choose which widgets stay on their desk after every boot.

Four questions

Answer with one letter each. The recommendation is marked and already picked below; tap another option to change it, then copy the answer.

1After the first sign-in, what should a new user see of Tips on the desk?

Why a: the card is the first thing a new person sees and shows what to ask without being asked; after the first week the desk is quiet again. A label is easy to miss on day one, and an app nobody knows about is never opened.

2When someone presses a tip’s button, such as “Make me a ticketing app”, what should happen?

Why a: pressing Enter themselves teaches where to type, lets them make the words theirs (“…for a team of four”), and never starts a minute-long build by accident. The voice thread’s empty-place buttons should then behave the same.

3How should someone choose which widgets are on their desk after every boot?

Why b: the owner asked that users can change widgets and set their defaults after boot; a card listing what exists is the visual way, the same shape as the AI card the stone opens. Nothing changes for anyone who never opens it.

4Should Tips replace the first-minute pieces that were designed but never built: the three example buttons above the empty pill and the ten-second tour?

Why a: none of those pieces exists yet, and two places suggesting things in the same first minute compete for the same glance. The faint line stays: it is the only place the Super key is written down.

The short answer: the desk after boot today

There are no widgets on the desk after boot, on purpose. The widgets brief, confirmed on 28 September, says a widget is present only while it has something to say, so a machine at rest is wallpaper and the pill. The code holds to it: the presence rules in shell/DeskState.qml, a test that a resting desk has no cards and no strips, and rail windows that are not even mapped.

What a user can change today

  • The word “desk” folds every card to a chip beside the pill, and back.
  • “show machine”, “hide watching” and the like show or put away one widget.
  • A sentence like “put watching on the right” goes to the agent’s desk tool, which works only when your own words asked for the desk.
  • All of it is kept in desk.toml, outside the restore points, so it survives a reboot and an undo never moves it. Needs you cannot be put away.

What a user cannot do today

  • Keep a widget up at rest, or see a list of the widgets there are.
  • Make a widget of their own (designed, not built).
  • Change anything with a click or a drag: the strips’ click and hover are not wired, and the desk thread is building dragging now.
  • Learn what to ask: there is no tips word, “what can you do” gets at most four plain lines from the model, and the example chips and ten-second tour exist on no branch.

Build first

Two pieces, each its own PR with its docs page.

01

Tips

large · five steps, each tested before the next
  1. Reduced motion everywhere (S). Theme.qml gains a writable reducedMotion, set by the shell and the app runtime from BOMBADIL_REDUCE_MOTION, which today reaches only the stone and the wallpaper. The desk’s fold, wash, ring and pill width honour it too.
  2. The tips table and tips.py (M). share/tips/tips.toml holds a row per tip. tips.py in agentd decides which tips this machine can back, keeps tips.json (first-ready date, days of use, tried and known ids), marks tips tried from agentd’s own live events with no model, and tells the shell only when something changes. The installer copies tips.json and desk.toml to the disk.
  3. Kit.Stone and Kit.TipScene (L). The stone moves into the kit with animate and ink. TipScene is pure QtQuick, plays a list of beats at app density and draws a still poster at card density, the way Kit.Diagram already serves both the shell and apps. Both stay internal: check_app refuses them in apps the agent writes.
  4. The Tips app and its words (M). A built-in kit app in share/apps/tips, opened by local words. Agent.draft(text) joins the kit: a one-shot summon that fills the pill, the Brain’s “Ask about this” made general.
  5. The Tips card on the desk (M). A tips entry in desk.py’s widgets at the top of the right rail and a TipsCard face, by the DESK.md recipe for a new widget. Its button sends summon with tip: true.
02

Your desk

medium · with the desk thread’s drag work

A built-in kit app, 960 × 516, sliding in from its drawer. It opens on “tips”, “open tips”, “show tips”, “what can you do”, “what can I ask” and “help”, and from the card. Selecting a tip plays it once and holds the last frame. Nothing loops, nothing advances by itself, and there is no sound.

What moves, and only this

  • The arrival, with the desk’s own fade and scale over 150 ms, once.
  • A 200 ms crossfade when you turn a tip, or 12 s after a tried tip’s turn ends.
  • The standard fold to its strip under windows.
  • No timer, no loop, no auto-advance and no scene on the desk. Under reduced motion every change is instant.

Where and when

  • The right rail’s top slot, the least urgent. Alone, it sits nearest the pill.
  • It appears only after setup, the persona card and the welcome line are done, and never first during a turn.
  • On screens under 1016 px it is only ever a strip.
  • Like every card it narrows the pill: to 710 px on a 1366 px screen for the first week.

The desk after boot

The rule stays, with one reading added: the desk shows only what has something to say. In your first week an untried Start-here tip is something to say, and a widget you keep always has something to say. Nothing is kept by default.

Tips leaves

  • when all six Start-here tips are tried,
  • after seven days on which you typed in the pill,
  • or at once on its × or “hide tips”, which answers “Put Tips away. Say tips to see them again.” That choice survives the install.

Unchanged

  • Machine still rises past a line; Watching still shows a recent failure; Needs you appears once the coding sessions land.
  • The desk you leave (put away, folded, rails, order) is the desk you boot to.
  • On the live USB every boot is a first boot, because nothing survives a restart.

Shaping your desk

Three states per widget: Keep (on the desk at rest, after every boot), When needed (today’s rule) and Off (put away). Only a widget with a calm face can be kept: today that is Tips and Machine. Now, Watching and Needs you have nothing to show when nothing happens.

The words, with no model

  • “keep machine” and “keep machine on the desk” keep it.
  • While Machine is kept, “hide machine” only stops keeping it: “Machine is no longer kept. It still comes up when something needs a look.” A second “hide machine” puts it away, as today.
  • “keep watching” answers “Watching shows only while something counts.” and changes nothing.
  • “widgets” opens the card. Each press changes desk.toml through the same path the words use, and the line says what changed. Nothing on the card is orange, because orange is the machine acting.
  • A kept Machine samples every 5 s, rounds its figures, and changes rows in place without motion. It reads “The machine is fine” until a line is crossed.
  • Moving is by sentence today, and by dragging a card by its title once the desk thread’s drag is proven.

Trying a tip

  1. Enter sends it as your own ask, with its restore point; Esc drops it; you can edit it first. A button ending in “…” leaves the cursor for your detail: “In Tickets, add…”.
  2. While the AI rests, model tips still fill the pill and Enter queues them as the waiting chip. Tips that need no AI (“How am I connected?”, “!uname -r”) run at once, offline.
  3. Tried comes from agentd’s live events with no model: the words sent equal the tip’s words, or the turn’s evidence matches the tip (a create_app, a local picture, a ! command, a stop, a job). A tip is never tried just because it was shown.
  4. 12 s after the turn ends the card crossfades to the next untried tip, and the app ticks the row.

How a tip moves

Storyboards, not video. Kit.TipScene draws each tip from a short list of beats with the real stone and faces redrawn from the theme tokens, so a tip is always in the current look, crisp at any size, and a few lines to add. Recorded clips are true by construction, but heavy, blurry at 300 px, stale whenever the look changes, and need a video player in the shell. Every scene on this page is drawn the same way.

BeatWhat it draws
keya keycap pressed: ⊞, ↵, Esc, Tab or !
typewords typing into the mini pill at 25 characters a second
stonethe stone’s face: rest, listening, working, needs you, done, stopped, resting
linethe line above the pill: text, tone, the amber or red edge, the command in mono, the seconds
windowan app window sliding in from its drawer, its rows, or a change to them
carda desk card: Watching, Now or Machine
picturea picture card above the line, boxes streaming in
foldthe desk’s cards folding to strips, or back
holda pause

Timing

  • 6 to 8 s, never over 10. Keys 300 to 400 ms; slides 300 ms on the system’s curve; fades 200 ms; the stone uses its own roll and hop.
  • Every scene ends on its payoff frame and holds it. That frame is the reduced-motion still and, in ink, the card’s poster.
  • Three phase captions per tip, at most three words each.

Truth

  • The mini stone rolls while the mini machine works, hops once when done and knocks when something waits, so a tip also teaches what the stone means.
  • Every string a scene shows is tagged template (checked against the real sentence in the code, so “Building Tickets, 140 lines” fails the test the day that line changes), typed, model, output or caption.
  • At rest the card has no timer and no running animation; measured with pidstat before shipping.

The first boot, minute by minute

On the live USB, on main plus this design. S is the moment sign-in completes.

  1. 0:00

    The ground colour. The wallpaper fades in once. The stone rolls orange beside “Starting”.

  2. 0:03

    “Which AI should run this computer?” with the Claude and Codex buttons; the stone knocks amber. No Tips card: setup owns the screen.

  3. sign-in

    The browser panel slides in and back out, 30 to 90 s.

  4. S

    “Signed in to Claude. Ask me for anything.” with a still green stone. The voice branch’s name card and hello, and the USB’s Install button, come here once built. Tips waits for all of them.

  5. S + 8 s

    The line fades. The faint first-day line sits just above the pill until the first Super or Esc. The Tips card arrives with the desk’s own motion, on “Say what you need. It gets made.” [Make me a ticketing app]. From here nothing moves.

  6. click

    A click on the picture slides the Tips app in and plays the tip once, about 7 s, then holds.

  7. press

    A press on the button fills the real pill. Enter runs the real thing at real speed.

  8. after

    Tickets probably covers the right rail, so the card folds to its “tips” strip. 12 s after the turn ends it turns to tip 2, “Ask for a picture, not a paragraph.”, which answers offline in under a second.

  9. installed

    The installed first boot is already signed in. The welcome-home line and the recovery key card come first; then the card, on the first tip not tried on the stick, and the undo tip appears in the app.

The seed: twenty tips

All backed by main today, each with its gate: a tip the machine cannot back is shown nowhere. Each picture is the scene’s last frame. Click one to play it in the app. Lines are proposals for the voice thread, in the teaching register: one line, no name, no “!”, no em-dash. {app}, {project} and {ai} are filled from the machine.

Waiting for their pieces: a coding session by name and the amber stone’s your-turn knock come with the coding-sessions branch; “What needs me in mail?” with the mail work; “noticed” with the self-improvement branch. Their rows ship with those PRs, gated until the code is on main.

How tips grow

  • Tips are rows, not code. The voice thread words them; tips.toml moves beside share/voice/lines.toml once the voice PR merges.
  • A PR that adds something a person can ask ships its tip row and beats in the same PR, the way it ships its docs, gated by a needs flag until its code is on main.
  • The tests fail a tip when its gate is unknown, its line breaks the rules or overflows 272 px at 13 px, its words reach nothing, its scene runs over 10 s, its poster fails to render, or a template string is not backed by the code.
  • A new kind of beat is a small piece of TipScene: session dots and the knock when the coding sessions land, the Mail window when Mail lands.
  • Order is fixed, never random: Start here, then the sections in order, untried first.
  • After an update, tips with new ids appear in the app’s New section only: no strip, no card, nothing unasked.
  • Later: tips per app from the apps’ [[does]] rows; search past about 40 tips; “Watch again” for real turns.

Tips’ own rules

  1. A tip is one line, one picture and the exact words.No paragraph anywhere. The line says what you get; the picture shows it; the button is the sentence you would type.
  2. A tip is true on this machine.It shows only what this machine can do now, as it really looks. A tip it cannot back is not shown, not even greyed.
  3. Nothing runs on a click.A tip’s button fills the pill; your Enter runs it, as your own ask with its restore point.
  4. Only the app moves.The desk card is a still picture. Scenes play inside the app you opened, once, and hold their last frame; every scene has a still version.
  5. It never speaks.No line above the pill, no mark on the stone, no sound, and never during a turn or over setup.
  6. It steps aside.After the first week it is gone from the desk unless you keep it; new tips after an update wait inside the app.
  7. Tips teaches what you have not asked; Noticed handles what you repeat.Tips never offers to make a word, an app or a widget from what you do. The agent never writes tips.

Changes to confirmed decisions

Each is named so the answers confirm it knowingly.

  • The rest state (widgets rule 4, identity rule 5, the voice brief’s cut of a standing welcome): Tips stands at rest during a new user’s first seven days of use, then leaves by itself. A widget the user keeps stands at rest only because they asked.
  • The voice brief’s cut of “tips of the day”: Tips never speaks on the line and never rotates. It is still a teaching surface shown unasked in the first week, which is what was asked for.
  • Strip-first for things raised unasked (self-improvement rule 2): Tips raises a full card in the first week. A new person’s first look is not a mid-task interruption. Question 1b keeps strip-first.
  • Desk rule 1 gains a ninth question, “What can I ask it to do?”, and Tips takes the right rail’s top slot until Noticed becomes a rail widget.
  • “Nothing else moves” (identity rule 7): scenes move only inside the Tips app you opened, once, with a still version. The card never animates.
  • Colour and the stone: the app stage shows the mini machine in its real colours, because it teaches what they mean; the card draws the stone in ink.
  • “No new words for the desk”: adds “tips”, “what can you do”, “what can I ask”, “help”, “widgets” and the verb “keep”, which gets one meaning, “stays on the desk at rest”.
  • Voice rule 5 (a chip sends its sentence): every tip button fills the pill and waits, and the empty-place chips follow the same rule, whichever is chosen.
  • The UX brief’s first minute: Tips replaces the unbuilt example chips and the ten-second tour (question 4); the habit chips are left to Noticed.
  • “A switch per widget”, turned down on 28 September: the Widgets card brings a per-widget choice back, because it has now been asked for; the shipped default stays the quiet desk.

Cut, and why

  • A carousel that turns by itself. It would be the one thing moving at rest, and motion here means the machine is working.
  • Animation on the desk card, even on hover. A poster says enough at a glance.
  • Page dots. Dots mean who on the desk.
  • A “2 new tips” strip after updates. New tips wait in the app.
  • Greyed “works once…” tips. A tip the machine cannot back is not shown.
  • Paragraphs, step lists and “learn more” pages. One line per tip.
  • Progress, streaks and “3 of 20 done”. The tick on a tried row is enough.
  • Tips spoken on the line, a mark on the stone, a sound. Tips never speaks.
  • The agent writing tips, or Tips offering to build from repeats. That is Noticed’s job.
  • Random order, and real figures in posters.

Risks, and what to check before building

  1. Three confirmed cuts are reversed (the rest state, tips of the day, strip-first). The fallback is question 1c, the app alone.
  2. The first tip is the slowest ask: a build takes about a minute, and a weak first app is the worst first impression. Tip 2 answers in a second. Test tip 1 on both providers.
  3. Focus handover: a press on the Bottom-layer rail window must hand the keyboard to the Overlay pill through summon, on one and two monitors.
  4. The pill narrows to 710 px at 1366 and 624 px at 1280 for the first week.
  5. Scene drift: the truth test catches changed wording, not changed window behaviour; model replies are only plausible.
  6. Tried detection misses paraphrases until the self-improvement ledger lands.
  7. Unmerged neighbours: the voice branch’s name card and hello come first; Tab and Enter on the empty pill stay reserved for the coding sessions’ walk.
  8. Measure the render cost of a still, mapped rail window on the VM’s software renderer and on real hardware, and the Tips app at 960 × 516 on a 1280 × 720 screen.
