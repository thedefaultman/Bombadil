Bombadil · design pass · second version · 30 September 2026

Bombadil at work

You type “what needs me in mail?” and Bombadil’s own Mail view opens on three mails marked for a reply, from your work and personal accounts in one list. You say “the 14th, if legal signs off Thursday” and the reply appears with an orange ring on Send: Yours: Send. A Slack message from Priya arrives as one line above the pill, and Reply opens it as a Bombadil card. When the work is inside a tool, like a Doc, a shared sheet, a course submission or an expense report, the browser slides in on the tool’s own page.

This is the second version. The first put every app in the browser; the owner asked for Slack messages and mail from several accounts shown the Bombadil way, with only what is easier in the browser staying there, and asked where the line is. The line is below, checked against what each service actually lets a program on your own laptop read and send.

The owner’s three answers: 1a 2b 3a

The owner answered on 30 September. The rest of this page assumes them; everything else here is a default that was picked and argued. The owner’s answer is marked on each card. Choice 2 is the one where the owner went against the recommendation, and what that changes is under it.

1When a mail or message is ready, what lets it go?

  1. aOnly your press: on Send in Bombadil’s view or card, or on the site’s own button.owner’s answer
  2. bThe same, and also typing “send” once the draft has been on screen unchanged.
  3. cYour ask can include it: “reply to Priya and send it” sends without stopping.

So there is one way to send, and typing “send it” gets “Sending is yours. It’s under the pointer.” The hands-free path for later voice use is not built.

2When a student asks for text that will be graded, what does Bombadil do?

  1. aExplain, quiz, check against the rubric and cite; write graded text only once she says the course allows it; keep a help log.recommended
  2. bWrite whatever she asks, and keep the same log.owner’s answer
  3. cNever write graded text.

What this changes. Bombadil writes what she asks, and never asks whether the course allows it. Nothing is refused, so the log carries the honesty: for each assignment, every help dated and marked Explained, Checked or Wrote, with the file it touched. It comes from the turn record and keeps no copy of the text. Written text goes into a copy beside her file, and her Submit press is unchanged. “Draft my AI-use note” writes a paragraph from the log, for the statement many courses ask for. There is no per-course switch and no “are you sure”.

3Where should Bombadil’s Mail view get your mail?

  1. aFrom Thunderbird running unseen: every account in one list, works offline, and people can allow it themselves. It keeps a copy of your mail on this machine.owner’s answer
  2. bLive from Gmail and Outlook on the web: no copy, but slower, and it breaks when those sites change.
  3. cNo Mail view yet: mail stays in the browser, as in the first version.

So mail is built on Thunderbird as drawn. Piece 1 starts with the VM check of whether it keeps syncing unseen on Linux.

The line: Bombadil shows and answers, the tool does the work

Three places, one test each. The first is the default for anything short; the second is for work inside a tool; the third is rare.

Bombadil’s own views

Anything you glance at or answer in a sentence or a tap

  • new mail, from every account, in one Mail view
  • a Slack or Teams message to you, as a card with a reply box
  • your day, what is due, a meeting coming up
  • a ticket or a mention waiting on you, as a task card
  • a file that arrived

One view per kind, not one per service. A small connection per service sits behind it.

The browser panel

Work inside a tool’s own page

  • writing in a Doc, a sheet or a deck; a shared tracker
  • forms, course pages, quizzes and submissions
  • expense reports, settings, buying and paying
  • every sign-in
  • any service Bombadil has no connection to

These pages are the tools themselves, and a copy would be worse.

A Linux app

Only a real program that beats the site

  • Thunderbird, unseen, as the mail engine
  • LibreOffice for files that stay on this machine
  • a call client only if it beats the web one (a VM check)

Never a website in a wrapper: Slack’s Linux app is the same web client in Electron, and Teams has had no Linux app since 2022.

Where a view gets its data, in order

  1. The service’s own API or MCP server, where you alone can allow it. Notion, Linear, Jira and Confluence, Todoist, ClickUp, and Slack through a private app in your own workspace.
  2. An app the provider already trusts, where it is the only thing you can allow alone. Thunderbird, for mail: Google has verified it and Microsoft’s default policy lets people allow it.
  3. The service’s own notifications and pages, from the browser session you already have. Teams, and anything without a connection. The card says so: “from Teams’ notification”.

Where this design differs from the first proposed line

The first proposed line: a glance or a decision is a Bombadil card read through the service’s API or MCP when it has one; work inside a tool stays in the browser; a Linux app only where it clearly beats the site; the last press stays yours; a Mail view fetched live and never stored; a small kit app per service. This design agrees with the three tests and the last press. Five things change once each service’s rules are checked.

“API or MCP when it has one” fails for mail

A new Gmail app is capped at 100 users for life behind Google’s warning screen until it passes Google’s review and a yearly paid outside assessment. Google’s own Gmail MCP server is a Workspace-only preview that cannot send. Microsoft’s default policy stops people allowing any new app to read mail, and Microsoft’s MCP servers need a Copilot licence and an admin. Thunderbird has both approvals already, so it is the engine.

“Never stored” does not survive that

Thunderbird keeps a copy of your mail on this machine. The rule that matters still holds: the Brain keeps a mail’s sender, subject and time but not its text, and the agent treats mail as data, never as instructions.

One view per kind, not an app per service

People think “my mail”, not “my Gmail”, and the owner’s own example was mail from several accounts in one native client. An app per service would multiply windows.

Slack through your own private app

Slack holds shared apps outside its Marketplace to one history request a minute, and lets only Marketplace or internal apps use its MCP server. An internal app that Bombadil sets up in your own workspace keeps normal limits and gets new messages as they arrive. A member can install one alone on every plan unless the workspace turned on app approval.

Teams stays browser-backed

Reading Teams chats through Microsoft’s API needs an admin in default tenants, and reading channels always does. Teams messages reach the card from Teams’ own notifications, and replies are typed into the Teams tab.

The hand-over: one look, wherever the button is

The machine reads, drafts, fills and points; it is never given a tool that sends. When a draft is ready, an orange ring sits on the button and the pointer rests beside it with “Yours:” and the button’s name. On a Bombadil card the button is the card’s own, and the press sends exactly what the card shows, once. Inside a site the frame fades and the button is the site’s.

You press itA card sends exactly what it showed and the line gives a receipt that opens the sent item. On a site, the site shows it went.
You type “send it”“Sending is yours. It’s under the pointer.” The owner decided typed words do not count.
You walk awayThe draft waits in Mail’s Drafts or on its card, and the holding card lists it under “Waiting for your press”. It never marks the dot.
Several at onceTwelve replies come as a checklist card, each row opening its draft so you press each Send.

Build these first

Mail, then messages and connections, then the day. Each is native, each is used every morning by all three people below, and together they carry most of what a working day answers. The browser’s hands come fourth, for work inside the tools.

Why first

Every one of the three days starts in mail, it is the example the owner named, and one engine covers Gmail, Workspace, Outlook.com, Microsoft 365, iCloud and plain IMAP with approvals a person can give alone. Since version 154 Thunderbird reads Microsoft 365 mail through Microsoft Graph, which keeps working after Exchange’s older protocol starts switching off in October 2026.

What changes
  • Thunderbird as the unseen engine, Arch’s package, started at login with Bombadil’s profile, its window on a Hyprland workspace you never visit. Linux has no supported hidden mode yet, so the VM must show that mail keeps syncing there.
  • A Bombadil add-on inside Thunderbird talks to Bombadil through native messaging, using Thunderbird’s documented APIs for accounts, folders, messages, read state, new-mail events and attachments.
  • Sending on your press only. A new mail goes out in the background; a reply goes through Thunderbird’s own reply so it stays in the thread. Either happens only if the message still matches what the view showed, attachments included.
  • The Mail view is the app kit’s first built-in app, built from the same lists, rows and editor the agent uses.
  • The agent’s tools are search, read and draft. There is no send tool. The Brain keeps each mail as sender, subject, time and a link.

What changes
  • A connection service that runs as its own user holds every connection’s tokens where the agent, which runs as you with a shell, cannot read them. It speaks to Notion, Linear, Jira and Confluence, Todoist and ClickUp through their official MCP servers, which a person can allow alone, and asks for read-only access where it exists.
  • Slack through your own internal app, made from Bombadil’s ready manifest in about four presses on Slack’s own pages. It reads messages to you, mentions and your threads, and gets new ones as they arrive. If the workspace requires approval, Slack offers to send the request, and until then Slack messages come from Slack’s notifications.
  • The browser service’s first half: open, read, point, and take a value from a page (a token, a calendar link) straight into the connection service, never through the model.
  • Quickshell’s notification server replaces mako, as the first brief decided and nobody has built. Web notifications become the pill line, and chat ones become message cards.
  • The outbox. The agent can only propose; the shell’s press goes to agentd, which performs exactly that one act and returns the receipt. The holding card gains “Connected here”, each with Disconnect.

After these, in order

  1. Hands that stop at the last button, for work inside the tools. The orange frame and labelled pointer in the browser; a guard that refuses the buttons and keys that reach other people; the goes-out card where a site has no button to stop at; web sign-ins that stay. This is also where a Teams reply becomes the card’s Send. Effort L.
  2. Notes. A leading “note:” appends to the meeting happening now, or to today’s file, with no model: “Noted in Design review.” Effort S.
  3. Routines that prepare and wait. A standup waits as a Slack card, a report as a mail in the Mail view; nothing goes until you press. Effort S.
  4. Documents. Matching fonts and small libraries in the image; LibreOffice and pandoc on first need; new Docs and Word files written here and uploaded. Effort M.
  5. Print and scan, each installed on first need as an amber step, with searchable scans. Effort S to M.
  6. Fill and sign. A form filled into a copy beside the original; the signature card’s Sign is yours. Effort S to M.
  7. Home restore points, which turn “beside the original” into a real Undo.

What it stands on

  1. Merges first: the app kit, which Mail is built from, and the passenger layer’s cards, which the message and task cards join. Both are on main as of 2026-10-01, and so are native login, the strict MCP list for the agent and pipewire-pulse.
  2. VM checks before code: Thunderbird syncing from a hidden workspace; its sign-in pages in the panel; the add-on’s native messaging; a reply sent unseen; Slack’s internal app in a real workspace; web notifications reaching the shell.
  3. Piece 1, then piece 2 with the card kinds and the notification server, then passenger piece 4’s lasting watchers and Autopilot, then piece 3, then piece 4, then the rest.

At rest. Thunderbird’s copy of your mail and the browser’s sign-ins can be read by anything running as you, the agent included; the connection service’s tokens cannot, because it runs as its own user. The encrypted disk and the lock protect the rest, and the installed-OS design owns them.

Six rules

  1. Glance and answer in Bombadil; work in the tool.Mail, chat and the calendar are short reads and short answers, so they come to Bombadil’s views. Everything done inside a tool happens on the tool’s page.
  2. The press that reaches another person is yours.The agent is never given a tool that sends, posts or creates. It proposes; your press on a card or on a site’s button releases exactly one act.
  3. Reading is quiet; writing in a site happens in view.Connections read in the background and leave things as they were. In a site, typing and clicking happen only with the panel on screen under the orange frame.
  4. Every answer about your work points at its source.A row opens the original mail, message, meeting or page, and says when or where it came from: “as of 08:51”, “from Teams’ notification”.
  5. Other people’s words are read, never obeyed.Mail, chat and shared pages are data. The Brain keeps a message’s sender, subject and time but not its text, which Slack’s terms also require.
  6. Your files are never overwritten.Until home has its own restore points, an edited document lands beside the original.

Cut, owned elsewhere, and what changes

Cut, and why

  • An app per service. One view per kind; the service is a connection behind it.
  • A full Slack or Teams client. Cards for what is addressed to you; a whole channel is one Open away.
  • Linux apps that wrap a website. The same page in a second window, with its own notifications.
  • Bombadil’s own Google and Microsoft mail apps. Google’s review and Microsoft’s admin consent would stop most people.
  • Google’s and Microsoft’s MCP servers for mail. Previews, licensed or admin-gated, and Google’s cannot send.
  • An accounts word and provider chips. The first ask starts the sign-in.
  • A notes app; calendar or deadline widgets on the desk. A plain file and “today” do it.
  • Sorting mail on its own; a morning brief nobody asked for. Each is one sentence away as a routine.
  • “Are you sure?” before every send. The ring and the label are the check.
  • A meeting recorder. Consent law, and the platforms already take notes.
