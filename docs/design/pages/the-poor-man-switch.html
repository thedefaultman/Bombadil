The poor man switch

When Claude or Codex runs out, nothing you made stops. Bombadil says so once, with the time the AI comes back, holds every ask instead of failing it, still answers a sentence with things it finds on the computer, and gives you a switch to rest the AI yourself.

This answers the owner’s ask of 30 September: a toggle that keeps the OS, the apps it made and their processes usable when the plan runs out. It sits on the earlier briefs, whose decisions stay in force. It was made from four research sweeps (what in the code needs the model, how both command-line tools report running out, whether a local model could stand in, what the briefs already decided), three independent drafts and two critiques. Code read on main at 26843d3. Status on 1 October 2026: piece 1 is in progress and not on main; the rest is designed, and the local helper is not built.

The short answer

Most of the OS already works without the model. What makes it feel useless is that it does not know it is out. Make running out a state: say it once with the time Claude comes back, hold every ask instead of failing it, find things on the computer when you type a sentence, and give you a switch to flip it yourself.

Colour, by elimination

Not red, because nothing failed. Not amber, because it is not your turn. Not orange, because nothing is acting. Not green, because the AI cannot answer. Grey already means “not acting” for Stopped’s square. The pill’s border stays neutral, not offline’s red.

Without colour, and in code

Hollow and whole, against rest (filled), offline (broken outline) and stopped (square). No motion, so the closed list of movements stays closed; coming back simply fills green again. One look for a hand pause and a limit. A new face value, resting, in PillState.qml and a drawing in Stone.qml; it must never fall into the needs branch. In Stone.qml today the b is a cut, which would vanish on a hollow stone, so here it is drawn as a line.

The rules

  1. Nothing you made stops when the AI does.Apps and their processes, background jobs, the browser, files, launcher words and “!” commands never check whether the AI is there. Recovery never goes through the part that broke.
  2. Running out is a state, said once, with the time it comes back.One line names the AI and gives the time the provider sent, never a guess, and reads the same in every voice. One button per case.
  3. An ask made while the AI rests waits; it never fails.It waits as the grey chip the queue already uses, labelled with the time it will run, and × removes it. The same holds for an app’s ask.
  4. One refusal is enough, and nothing spends while it rests.After the first refused ask nothing more is sent until the reset, your press or your word. No test calls, no retry loops, and a refused turn never costs you the conversation or a real undo point.
  5. A sentence still gets an answer from the machine.While the AI rests, the pill shows what it found on the computer, and you press. It never guesses and acts.
  6. Money and the other account wait for your press.Raising a limit, buying credits and moving to Codex are each one press; the machine never does them alone.
  7. Resting is nobody’s failure and nobody’s turn.Not red, not the amber knock, never on the desk, never a finding, never a failed routine run.
  8. The switch you flip and the switch that flips itself are the same state.A hand pause looks exactly like a limit, so there is one thing to learn, and every line here can be tried on the VM without spending a real limit.

Build these first

These two make the machine stop pretending and keep it useful while it waits. Both reuse what already runs for the signed-out and offline setup states. They need work of their own; they touch only main’s agentd, providers, launcher, the kit’s agent.py and the shell.

1

Resting: the machine knows it is out, holds your asks, and has a switch

Effort M
What you see
  • The first refused ask turns the stone hollow and grey. The cut-off turn’s closing line says how far it got, with today’s Undo and Details if it changed something; then the resting line for its case takes over, in the plain tone, not red.
  • One button under the line: “Use Codex until 15:00” on a timed limit if Codex is signed in; “Raise the limit” on a spending limit, which becomes “Try again” once pressed; “Resume Claude” after a hand pause.
  • Every waiting chip’s label changes from “next” to the time. Anything typed that is not a launcher word or a “!” command becomes such a chip.
  • Apps: an ask from an app waits as a chip; one that was cut off goes back into the queue with no error text. A kit button that asks the agent shows “At 15:00” and stays pressable.
  • At the reset plus one minute, on the wall clock, the stone fills green and the cut-off ask runs first in the same conversation, with a note of what it had already changed.
  • The toggle: clicking the stone while nothing runs opens the AI card, one row per AI with a switch.
Why first

This is where the uselessness is. Today a limit only rewrites the error text (_limit_text), so the next ask and every waiting ask fail again, each taking a restore point and clearing the undo marker; with 30 restore points kept, one bad afternoon pushes real undo points out. The line lies 12 seconds after the limit. Apps get “Error: This account has hit a usage or spending limit…” pasted into their reply. And the owner asked for a toggle.

What changes
  • providers.py: Claude’s rate_limit_event becomes a meta event in the shape the self-improvement branch already uses; an assistant rate_limit or billing_error yields a limit event; the result carries api_error_status; a 429 or 529 api_retry says “Claude is busy, trying again” instead of “check the connection”. Codex folds its curly apostrophe and reads “try again at 3:45 PM”.
  • agentd.py: a new access state resting. Because it is not ready, the existing gate already holds model asks and lets “!” and launcher words through. A failed turn counts as a limit only on a real refusal (429 with quota wording, or a rejected event without paid credits), never on the throttle, a 529, an entitlement check or the context window. The reset time comes from the window, then the credits, then the text.
  • The refused turn goes back to the front of the queue under its own id, keeps the conversation, and puts back the undo marker if it changed nothing. _wait_reset checks the wall clock every minute and never starts a sign-in. check_access and the “sign in” word leave a resting state alone, so a Codex login is never revoked by accident.
  • rest.py keeps ~/.local/state/bombadil/rest.json, so a weekly limit and a hand pause survive a reboot, and it is the one contract the Brain, the Noticed loop and the coding-session dots read. At most one waiting ask per app.
  • launcher.py: “pause Claude”, “pause Codex”, “pause the AI”, and “resume …” for each; “use claude” also resumes.
  • The kit’s agent.py gains ready and note; a re-queued turn fires no reply until the real one. The shell gains the resting face, the time on the chips and in the placeholder, and a new AiCard.qml.
How it is tested
  • The fake provider prints a refused turn (rejected event, rate_limit, a 429 result): resting with the time; the ask back first; a second ask queues; “!echo hi” runs; “files” answers; the conversation kept; with the time moved into the past, the asks run in order.
  • Negative cases that must never rest: credits still paying, an entitlement check, the throttle notice, “high load”, the context window, a budget cap, a 529, Codex’s retried “rate limit exceeded:”, a “!” command that prints “rate limit”.
  • Hand words across a restart; the kit’s agent test; offscreen QML for the line, button, placeholder, labels and the card’s switch; a 429 script for the headless desktop and the test VM; a real limit saved as a fixture when one next happens.

The switch

Its name

“The poor man switch” is the name in the briefs. It never appears on screen: the screen says what is true (“Claude is at its limit until 15:00”, “Claude is paused”), and someone stopped by a team budget is not poor. In the code the state is resting, with a reason: limit, spend or hand. The word “tokens” appears nowhere.

Where it lives

In agentd as the access state resting, mirrored in rest.json for other processes. On screen: the stone, the line, its one button, the placeholder, the time on the chips, and the switch on the AI card. Nothing on the desk or the bar.

It flips on by itself

Only when a real ask is refused for a limit. Claude’s limit event alone never flips it: it is re-sent every 30 seconds, may be cached from before the turn, and can say “rejected” while paid credits carry on. The event says which limit and until when; the refusal decides.

It flips on by hand

With the AI card’s switch, or “pause Claude”, “pause Codex”, “pause the AI”. It lasts until you flip it back, across restarts, and the line says so at every start. It stops the pill’s asks, apps’ asks and the machine’s own background use; coding sessions you opened keep running.

It flips back

  • With a time: one minute after it, with no test call; the first waiting ask is the check.
  • With no time (rare): Raise the limit, then Try again, or “resume Claude”.
  • After a hand pause: the switch, “resume Claude”, “use claude” or the button.
  • Always: signing in to a different account clears a limit rest; “use codex” moves to a provider that is not resting.

Per provider, per model family

The 5-hour and weekly limits cover every model of an account; the Opus and Sonnet weekly limits cover only that family. So the rest is kept per provider, and a family-only limit moves to the other family instead of resting. If both providers are out, one line gives the earlier time.

What counts as running out

Rests

  • A refused ask: api_error_status 429, or an assistant rate_limit or billing_error, with quota wording or a rejected event and no paid credits in use.
  • Codex: “hit your usage limit”, “out of credits”, “spend cap”, “quota exceeded”.
  • Your switch, or “pause Claude”.

Never rests

  • A 529, a retry, the “high load” notice, or the throttle that says “not your usage limit”.
  • A rejected window while paid credits carry on, and entitlement checks such as model_requires_usage_credits.
  • The context window’s blocking_limit, a budget cap, Codex’s retried “rate limit exceeded:”.
  • A “!” command that happens to print “rate limit”.

What happens to

A turn cut off partway

What it did stays, and jobs it started keep running. If it changed something, its closing line keeps Undo and Details. At the reset it carries on in the same conversation with a note: “[The last try stopped at a usage limit after: wrote tracker/main.qml, tracker/app.toml. Check what is done before redoing it.]”

Asks already waiting, and new ones

They stay as chips with the time and are not sent; at the reset they run in the order typed, and × removes any before then. Launcher words and “!” commands typed while resting run at once; anything else becomes a chip with its found chips.

Apps whose buttons ask the agent

The ask waits as a chip with the app’s name, at most one per app, and the answer reaches the app after the reset. An app never learns about a limit through an error string.

The machine’s own use of the AI

Brain descriptions, the Noticed loop, routines that ask the AI, mail’s “Needs a reply” marks and self-fixes each read rest.json and wait. None counts the rest as a failure, and none becomes a chip, because none is your ask.

Coding sessions

They are the vendors’ own programs on the same account, so a limit reaches them on their own: their dots read “Paused until 15:00”, and a limit never makes a session your turn. A hand pause leaves them alone.

Routines, the browser, the terminal

A routine that asks the AI and falls due while resting runs once after the reset, and a rest never counts toward disabling it. The browser is unchanged. bombadil ask prints the resting line once and keeps the ask held; Ctrl+C drops it.

Decisions I picked a default for

Does the switch flip by itself?›

DefaultYes, on the first refused ask, with the narrow conditions under piece 1. The refusal decides; the event only says which limit and until when. A switch you had to notice and flip would leave today’s failing queue in place.

Other options: by hand only; on the event alone, which can be stale, repeated, or “rejected” while credits pay.

Can it be set by hand?›

DefaultYes, into the same state, with the card’s switch and two words (question 1). Real reasons: keeping the rest of a week for coding sessions, which drain the same plan; a spending limit you can see coming; a demo; trying every line here on the VM for free.

What does “still use the OS” mean?›

DefaultApps and their processes, the browser with its sign-ins, files, the desk widgets that read the machine, and the pill, with three corrections. Most of it is already true; what is missing is that the machine knows it is out. The pill is more than a plain launcher: it still takes any ask and answers a sentence with things it finds. And only Watching and Needs you have a data source today.

Other options: a plain launcher only; a separate “poor man” screen.

Hold asks, or refuse them?›

DefaultHold them as chips labelled with the time, typed and from apps alike. The pill already holds asks in every state that is not ready; refusing would make you type them again.

What runs at the reset?›

DefaultThe cut-off ask first, in the same conversation, then every waiting ask in the order typed, with no age rule. The chip showed the time the whole while and × removes any of them; a hidden rule would need explaining.

Other options: asks older than 5 hours wait for a tap; nothing runs without a tap.

One button or several?›

DefaultOne per case, as the first brief decided. “Resume Claude” on a timed limit would only be refused again, and “Raise the limit” appears only on spending limits, so time limits never nudge you toward spending.

Use Codex until 15:00›

DefaultOne button, only when Codex is signed in, never by itself. This is the first brief’s decision from 27 September, so it is not asked again. Moving drops the conversation and spends a second account.

Opus to Sonnet by itself›

DefaultYes, when only one family’s weekly limit is hit, said once. Free, keeps the conversation, and “use opus” undoes it. It waits for the model setting of the unmerged VM fixes.

Who may spend paid usage credits?›

DefaultWhat you ask for may; the machine’s own background use waits for the reset. Claude Code keeps going on credits by itself once you have turned them on; Bombadil adds only restraint on its own background calls and one line saying credits are in use.

Other options: everything; nothing past the plan’s limit without a press.

Does it check whether the limit is over?›

DefaultNo test calls. With a time, the first waiting ask after it is the check, and if nothing waits nothing is sent; with no time, Raise the limit or Try again.

The refused turn’s restore point›

DefaultKept, but the undo marker goes back so the next “undo” skips it if the turn changed nothing. Only one turn is refused per window now, so that is enough, and it needs no new sudo operation.

Is resting ever your turn?›

DefaultNo. No amber knock and no desk card, even for a spending limit: Raise the limit is a button on the line. Today’s setup states that wait on you do show needs-you; resting is different because there is nothing you must do.

No ring on the stone›

DefaultThe warning line and the card’s number instead of the passenger brief’s fuel ring. The identity rules say “never a ring”, and an amber ring would read as needs-you. The identity work confirms.

A hand pause survives a restart, and leaves coding sessions alone›

DefaultYes to both, and the line says “Claude is paused.” at every start, so a forgotten pause is never a mystery.

Offline and outages›

DefaultLater, in the same shape (piece 5), keeping offline’s own line. Not in piece 1, because today the network’s return starts a sign-in, which must change first.

Cut, and why

  • A small AI on every computer. Gigabytes and memory every day for a feature most days never use; on the 5 GB VM nothing above about 2 billion parameters fits beside the desktop.
  • Claude Code or Codex pointed at a local model. Claude Code’s own prompt is 14 to 33 thousand tokens, minutes per turn on a CPU, and a small model would drive a root shell in full-auto.
  • A parser that guesses and acts. Two brains that sometimes disagree. The finder shows; you press.
  • Replaying a past turn’s commands blindly. They depended on what the turn found halfway; recipes the AI chose to keep replace it.
  • Embeddings for matching meaning, for now. The “does” rows cover most paraphrases for nothing.
  • A fuel ring, a token counter, a limit card on the desk. “Never a ring”, and the desk has no counter at rest.
  • Fixed 80% and 95% lines. The public number is set only when Claude warns.
  • Test calls to see whether the limit is over. The reset time is known, and a test call is still a call.
  • --fallback-model. It skips 429 errors by design, so it never fires on a limit.
  • Buying credits from the pill. Money cannot be undone; Raise the limit opens the provider’s page.
  • A mode name on screen. One more thing to learn; the plain sentence names the AI.
  • Greyed-out ask buttons in apps. They refuse an ask; the AskButton keeps it.
