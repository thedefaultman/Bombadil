The Bombadil Mark

You liked the pebble best and asked for it to be more distinct and to have some personality. Here are three ways to make the stone Bombadil’s own, each drawn in every state of the pill, at every size and in place. All three give the stone the same character in motion, and all three spell the name in B’s drawn letters with a small stone for the dot on the i.

The character all three share

Round one’s stone only tumbled. Now it behaves like a small, cheerful stone with a mind of its own, and each thing it does means one thing, so the states read without colour. Drawn here with E; D and F do the same. With reduced motion on, working falls back to today’s pulse, Needs you holds its glow, and nothing else moves.

RestSits still on its flat side. At rest the screen stays as quiet as today.
ListeningNew. While you type, it leans a little toward your words and stays leaning.
WorkingRolls a third of a turn every second, and every beat ends exactly where it started. Here the b holds still while the stone turns around it; D and F roll inside their own square, touching all four sides.
Needs youKnocks twice, tipping on its base and landing back, then waits, with a soft amber glow that breathes. Never a ring.
DoneOne small hop, a squash as it lands, then still.
StoppedThe Stop button’s own square, in grey. Never orange, because orange means working.
OfflineOnly its broken outline is left, in red: nothing there.
D

The merry pebble

C’s stone exactly as you saw it, flat side down and drawn in ink, given the character above. It gets its identity from the company it keeps: the lockup, the download page and the README always show it beside the name, whose i is dotted with the same stone, until the stone alone is enough.

Why it works

The smallest step from the one you liked, the quietest pill (a 12 px stone where today’s 10 px dot sits), and the clearest shape at 16 px.

Its weak spot

Seen on its own it is still a rounded triangle: a guitar pick, a gumdrop, and close to Vercel’s triangle at 16 px on a dark tile. The character only shows while it moves, so a screenshot, a USB stick or an app icon does not yet say Bombadil.

E

The riding b

Chosen

The stone with a lowercase b cut clean through it. When the machine works, the stone turns around the b while the b stays level and still, like a hub that never turns: you ride level while the machine does the work underneath. The b sits inside the circle that every pose of the stone covers, so the cut never breaks the edge; in the pill at least 0.9 px of stone stays around it.

Why it works

It says Bombadil wherever it appears, on its own: the app tile, the stick, GRUB’s screen and the pill. It keeps what you liked about C (the shape, the roll, the calm), and the motion shows the idea in one glance: the letter holds still while the stone works.

Its weak spot

The pill’s mark grows from today’s 10 px dot to an 18 px stone with a letter in it, so the resting pill is a little louder. A letter on an orange shape sits near Blogger’s orange B, so the stone is only orange while it turns, never as a still logo. At 16 px the b’s bowl is about 2 px across; it holds at 1x, but only just.

F

The stone b

The stone as the bowl of a lowercase b, beside a straight stem. While the machine works it rolls against the stem, always touching the stem and the ground, and when it needs you it knocks on the stem. In the name it is the first letter, so the lockup reads bombadil with a stone for its first bowl.

Why it works

The most playful of the three and the best lockup: the stone is part of the name itself, and knocking on the stem is the clearest Needs you.

Its weak spot

On its own the b has a pointed bowl that reads as an l beside a fin, and at 16 px it comes apart into a bar and a triangle. In the pill the stem stands right before the text, where it can read as the text cursor. Turned with the stone pointing left or right it becomes a media player’s skip button, so it only ever stands corner up.

E, in place

The places the installed-OS brief left for the mark, drawn with E. D and F drop into the same places one for one: their lockup on the download page and in the README, their mark on GRUB’s screen and the cards, their stone in the greeter.

The orange

Bombadil's accent, the colour that means “the machine is acting”, is #d97757. That is exactly the fill of Claude's own logo (claude.ai's favicon), and Anthropic's brand uses it as their primary accent. Bombadil also runs Codex, so an orange mark would read as Claude's and would be wrong half the time.

The identity, in eight rules

These hold whichever mark you pick. They come from the look on screen today and from decisions earlier briefs already made.

  1. The stone is the machine.The dot in the pill is the one piece of Bombadil everybody sees every day. The stone takes its place and does what the dot did, with a character.
  2. The mark is ink; colour is the state.Drawn in #e6e8eb on the dark ground and #15181b on a light page. The stone takes a colour only in the pill, and only the colour of its state; the one fixed touch of orange is the dot of the i. Bombadil is recognised by a shape, never by an orange blob.
  3. Colour says who, everywhere.White is you, orange is the machine acting, blue is a coding session. Amber is a step that touches the system or something waiting on you, red is failed, offline or can't be undone, green is connected.
  4. Every state reads without colour.Rest is still, listening leans, working rolls, needs you knocks and glows, done hops once, stopped is a square, offline is a broken outline.
  5. At rest, quiet.The screen at rest stays wallpaper and one pill with a still stone. No splash, no logo wallpaper.
  6. Things that speak are round; things that hold have 12 px corners.The pill, the line and the chips are fully round; windows, cards and panels are 12; controls are 8.
  7. Every movement means one thing.Rolling is working, a knock is waiting on you, a hop is done, a lean is listening. Nothing else moves, and each has a still version for reduced motion.
  8. Original.Nothing from Tolkien's text or art (no hat, feather, boots, ring, runes or song lines), no face and no mascot. The character is in how the stone moves, never in a face.

The design system

The look is already a system; it is written in two places that have drifted. The component kit's Theme.qml names about forty tokens, and the shell draws the pill, the line and the chips with 22 hard-coded colours, 9 of them in no token, and no font family at all. The system is the kit's tokens, kept, plus the few the shell needs, so both read one file. Nothing is restyled: every value is on screen today.

The full system is published on its own: every token with its use, the type scale, and the kit's 43 components, each drawn with notes on when to use it.

Cut, and why

  • The pill as a logo. A rounded bar with a dot reads as a toggle switch, and as Blogger's B.
  • The hop and the walk as marks. The hop was a stock trajectory pictogram; the walk was a third b-and-dot whose gap closed at small sizes. The hop lives on as Done.
  • An orange mark, or a mark on an orange or red tile. Claude's colour, and the territory of Beats, Blogger and Ubuntu.
  • A blend of B and C. You asked for the pebble; B gives only its letters.
  • The stone b turned on its side. With the stone pointing left or right, stem and stone read as a media player’s skip button.
  • A bare letter in the pill. A letter alone in the slot reads as a typed character. E’s b is cut through a stone, so it reads as a mark.
  • An amber ring for Needs you. Next to the name Bombadil a gold ring is the One Ring. A soft filled glow instead.
  • A boot splash. The installed-OS brief cut Plymouth; GRUB's still screen carries the mark, then the pill rolls and says Starting.
  • A logo wallpaper. The ground stays plain #101214; rest means nothing on screen but the pill.
  • Tolkien imagery and a mascot. No hat, feather, boots, ring, runes, song lines or face.
