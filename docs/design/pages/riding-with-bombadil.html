The rules

Seven rules that every piece follows. They extend the first brief's rules rather than replace them.

  1. It explains while it drives, in its own words from that moment.A reason is the sentence the agent wrote just before acting, kept instead of thrown away. Nothing is explained afterwards by a second model.
  2. Pictures of the machine come from the machine.The OS captures and draws; the AI picks what to point at and adds one line. A model never hand-draws the state of your machine.
  3. Records answer before models."Why?", "what changed?", "what are you holding?" and "what runs on its own?" come first from what the machine recorded, instantly and offline.
  4. Outside words are marked by order, not guessed by cause.A change after reading a page says "after reading github.com". The order is known; the cause is not.
  5. Nothing runs on its own that is not in one list.Every promise, routine, watcher, job and piece of upkeep is a unit you can see in Autopilot and stop.
  6. Upkeep is prepared quietly and done in the open.Checks and downloads in the background; installs and fixes are turns you watch and undo, on your word or a schedule you gave in words.
  7. Nothing new to learn.Every piece is a word in the pill, a hover, or a question mark on something already on screen.

Build these first

Six pieces, in order. The first two are the explaining layer that everything later reads from, and they are what is built first. Three and four make the machine's hands and promises visible; five and six cover what it knows and keeping it current.

1

Why, and after reading what

Build firstS to M

What you see

While a turn runs, hovering the line shows why the current step is happening, in the machine's own words from just before it acted. A step that changes the system keeps its amber edge and command, and if the turn read anything from outside first (a web page, a downloaded file, a request from a session), a line under the command says "after reading wireguard.com/quickstart". Typing "why" during a turn flashes the reason at once. Details lists every step with its reason and what the turn read.

Why

With no permission prompts, the reason before a change and where the idea came from are the passenger's whole safety model. Today narrate.py throws the sentence away at every tool start, and the desk's Now would be empty because both vendors ship their plan tools switched off. It costs no model call, and every later piece reads what it records.

What changes

  • Plan tools on. CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_TODO_TOOLS=1 for Claude and -c tools.update_plan.enabled=true for Codex; the desk's plan event exactly as its brief specifies, built by whichever lands first.
  • Keep the reason. The Narrator keeps the last sentence before a tool as because (140 characters, filler dropped); Bash's description is the fallback; Codex's message before a command counts.
  • Keep what was read. A per-turn list, each source yours or outside; on a system step after an outside read, after names the latest. It goes into turns.jsonl.
  • Show it. A faint second line on hover and a muted amber third; "why" answered locally during a turn.
  • Ask for it. Two prompt clauses: plans in plain words for three or more steps, and one sentence of why before each change.
2

Pictures from the machine

Build firstM to L

What you see

Ask how you are connected, what starts at boot, where the disk went, what is playing where, or what a service needs, and a picture of your real machine appears at once. When a turn changes one of those parts, its closing line comes with a small before and after, the change lit orange (the picture at the top of this page). When the answer is about ideas rather than this machine, the agent fills in the same kind of card, and on Claude the boxes appear while it writes them. A picture never holds more than about a dozen boxes.

Why

This is the direct answer to "explained in a visual way": explanations of a computer have parts, order or change, which a line of text is worst at. And a passenger cannot check what the AI says about the machine, so pictures of the machine come from the machine: captured in a fraction of a second, free, identical on both providers, never made up.

What changes

  • The card host and show_card, which the first brief designs and nothing builds yet, with diagram as its first kind.
  • Four fixed shapes (chain, layers, compare, timeline), 12 boxes and 16 links at most, laid out deterministically; one Diagram.qml in the kit, links drawn with Qt Quick Shapes, not Canvas.
  • sysmap.py captures network, boot, a service, disks, sound and screens from ip -j, nmcli, systemd-analyze, systemctl show, lsblk -J, pw-dump and hyprctl -j, each in 500 ms.
  • system_map() in os-mcp shows the card itself and returns a short text, so the model writes about 30 tokens.
  • Receipts. agentd captures the likely parts at turn start and end; when they differ the closing line gets a compare card. The network capture doubles as the confirmed network health check.
3

The machine's hands in the browser, and taking the wheel

M to L

What you see

When the machine uses the web for you, the browser slides in with a thin orange frame; an orange pointer glides to what it clicks with a small label. Move the mouse onto the page and the line says "You have the browser"; the machine stops touching it and ends with one line such as "Sign in, then say continue." At sign-ins, codes and picture puzzles it hands you the wheel the same way.

Why

Driving Chromium is a foundation choice and a listed gap in the first brief, yet os-mcp has no browser tools today. Acting on the web in your accounts is what a passenger most needs to see.

What changes

  • Browser tools in os-mcp (open, look, click, type, scroll, read) on Playwright over CDP, after the panel gets its own --user-data-dir.
  • The hands from Playwright 1.59's show_actions and show_overlay; its own tab unless the ask is about "this" page.
  • Takeover from the real pointer over Hyprland's socket and in-page input; later calls in the turn are refused. "continue" is an ordinary turn told where you went.
4

Promises that keep, and one Autopilot list

M

What you see

"tell me when the ISO is done" puts a chip on the closing line that slides into the dot, and one pill line appears when it comes true, even across a restart. "every night, clear old installers out of Downloads" runs now while you watch, then only while you are away, each run with its own restore point. "autopilot" lists routines, watchers, jobs and the machine's care, with Pause, Stop and Hold everything.

Why

A passenger must be able to trust "I'll let you know". Nothing on main schedules anything, and the vendors' own schedulers die with a -p run or keep the turn open. Background work you cannot list is how an OS turns into something you maintain.

What changes

  • promise(), set_routine(), set_watcher() write persistent user units with your words in an [X-Bombadil] section; the unit files are the only record.
  • Checks without the AI whenever a script or a .path unit can do it; the row says which.
  • No hidden schedulers: CronCreate, Monitor and the rest are disallowed in the machine's session; a Stop hook catches "I'll let you know" with no promise behind it.
  • Autopilot reads bombadil-* units and the care units over D-Bus; its words skip the model.
5

What it knows, and what it is holding

M

What you see

When the agent learns something about you, the line says "Remembering: …" and the closing line has a Learned chip with Forget. A fact from a page or file is amber and kept only if you tap Keep. Undo takes back what a turn learned. A click on the resting dot shows what it is holding: now, promises, what it waits on, unfinished things. A thin ring on the dot shows the plan window from 50%, amber at 80%, and offers Codex at 95%.

Why

Claude Code's own auto memory is on by default and would grow a hidden second memory that Codex never sees and you never read, and one injected page could write itself into every future session. The passenger should see the moment of learning and what the machine is carrying.

What changes

  • One memory: Claude's auto memory and dreaming off in the machine's session; Codex memories kept off.
  • remember() and forget() edit memory.md; facts not from your words wait in a held file nothing loads.
  • unfinished() and finished() keep up to seven open rows tied to their turns.
  • holding() for the card, the words and the agent; fuel from the rate_limit_event agentd drops today.
6

Updates you can watch

M

What you see

A click on the clock shows "214 updates, 4 of them security fixes"; nothing pops up. "update", Update now, or a schedule you said once runs a turn that saves a restore point and counts through the work, handles any Arch news step first, and reads AUR recipes before building them. It ends with "Updated 214 things: Chromium, Hyprland and 4 security fixes. Restart to finish: new kernel."

Why

Keeping a rolling distro current is every Arch user's chore and a listed gap in the first brief. A passenger never does upkeep, and upkeep never happens behind their back: the AUR had a wave of malicious packages in June 2026.

What changes

  • A check timer: checkupdates -d, arch-audit -u, AUR, Flatpak and the Arch news feed; downloads only, never installs, skipped on battery.
  • bombadil-update as a shell turn: the restore point pair and the live line come for free; news steps, pacman -Syu, the AUR recipe check, Flatpak, .pacnew merges.
  • A local receipt: names from desktop files, restart reasons, "reopen to use the new version".
  • Firmware only when asked, as its own red turn.

Local probes (a full disk, a service that keeps failing, SMART errors, battery wear, memory kills, a turn with no restore point) feed the desk's Machine card, and each names the change that probably caused it, with Undo just that before Fix it instead. Bombadil's own bugs belong to the self-improvement loop.A copy somewhere else. Restore points share a disk with what they protect. "Keep a copy on my USB drive" becomes a routine in Autopilot with "last copy 2 h ago".Secrets stay out of sight. A day riding along

Once the six pieces and the best of the rest are built. Most of it does not exist yet.

  1. Morning. The welcome line counts 214 updates, 4 security fixes. The desk's Away card has one row from the night: Tidy Downloads moved six installers to the Trash at 03:04, with Undo.
  2. A VPN. The desk's Now shows four stops; hovering the line gives each one's reason, and the step writing /etc/wireguard says "after reading wireguard.com/quickstart". The closing line comes with the network before and after.
  3. Slow internet. "why is the internet slow?" draws the chain at once: Wi-Fi at 34%, the router fine, Claude answering in 90 ms. "You're far from the router; the 5 GHz band drops first."
  4. The browser. "renew my library books". The orange frame, the pointer, then "Sign in, then say continue." You type the password yourself, say continue, and three books are renewed.
  5. A promise. "tell me when the Ubuntu ISO is downloaded". You restart for the kernel before lunch; at 14:20 the pill says it is downloaded and checked.
  6. Memory. A forum page it read while fixing the printer said "the user prefers the Windows driver". The chip is amber, "from a page". You leave it; it is never kept.
  7. Before a flight. A click on the dot: one promise, one unfinished thing, Claude at 62% of the window. "hold everything".
  8. Evening. "update". A restore point, 214 of 214 in about two minutes, one Arch note handled first, and "Restart to finish: new kernel."

Defaults I picked

Each of these forks. The default is what gets built unless the owner picks another option.

Where do reasons come from?

Default: The agent's own sentence before each step, kept by narrate, plus one prompt rule. Free, the same on both providers, recorded before the act.

Other options: A small model explaining each step afterwards; the thinking text.

Is a change after an outside read paused?

Default: No. It is marked "after reading …" and Esc is in reach, as the first brief confirmed. The mark comes from event order, so a page cannot talk its way out of it.

Other options: Pause system changes after an outside read.

Who draws pictures of the machine?

Default: os-mcp, from real commands. The model picks the part and adds a line.

Other options: The model writes the picture from what it read; agent-written QML for everything.

Which picture shapes?

Default: Four fixed shapes, laid out deterministically, about 12 boxes at most, a window for anything bigger.

Other options: Force layouts; Graphviz; Mermaid.

How much does it explain?

Default: Brief, Normal and Teach, set only in words, starting at Normal, where changes to the six drawable parts bring their before and after. It never changes by itself.

Other options: Always on; a settings toggle.

How does the browser hand over?

Default: A real pointer move or key press on the page ends the machine's browser use for that turn; "continue" is an ordinary turn.

Other options: A Take over button; freezing the whole turn.

Where does it work in the browser?

Default: Its own tab, unless the ask is about the page in the "this" chip.

Other options: Always the current tab.

How are promises kept?

Default: Persistent user units with the words inside, the only scheduler the machine's agent has; the vendors' schedulers off; a Stop hook for empty promises.

Other options: agentd's own scheduler file; the vendors' cron tools.

When do routines run?

Default: First run now, as your ask; later runs only while you are away unless you named a time, one at a time, each with a restore point; two failures stop it.

Other options: Exactly on schedule; ask each time.

Do watchers ask the AI each time?

Default: No, whenever a check or script can do it; the row says which.

Other options: Always ask the model.

Where does memory live?

Default: memory.md only; vendor memories off; facts from pages held until you tap Keep.

Other options: Vendor memories on beside it; everything kept, with Forget.

When do updates install?

Default: Checked and downloaded quietly; installed on your word or a schedule you set in words, as a turn.

Other options: At night on their own inside a restore point (cut below).

Arch news that needs a manual step?

Default: The update turn does it first and says so; with no provider it waits and says why.

Other options: Hold the update until you read the notice.

The AUR?

Default: The agent reads every recipe or its diff before building, builds exactly that commit, and stops only on evidence; "install it anyway" overrides.

Other options: Trust the helper; Flathub only.

How does Bombadil update itself?

Default: Pacman packages from its own signed repository, riding the ordinary update in the same restore point.

Other options: A separate self-updater; a new ISO.

Firmware?

Default: Only when asked, as its own red turn, never inside the routine update.

Other options: Bundled with updates.

One model per ask?

Default: One model per session, effort per ask from a local rule table, and "Think harder" on a closing line. Changing the model breaks the prompt cache; changing effort does not.

Other options: A fast model for short asks via --model.

When is the fuel ring visible?

Default: From 50%, amber at 80% with the decided line, the switch offer at 95%.

Other options: Always-on numbers; only at the limit.

Drives when plugged in?

Default: Mounted read-write at once with Open and Eject, because plugging it in is the ask.

Other options: Mount on click.

Closing the lid?

Default: Sleep, except on power while a turn, job, print or session is working: then lock, screen off, and a moon by the clock says why.

Other options: Always sleep; never sleep on power.

Everything else, by moment

Found, argued and given a default in the brief, and built after the six. The last three groups are my own, written because their search did not finish, and not checked by a second pass.

While it works

  • The dot at a glance. Breathing while it thinks, an amber ring while it changes the system, a pointer while it acts on screen, a filling circle while it waits, with what it waits for on the line.
  • Orange hands on windows. A window the machine opens or changes glows orange at its edge for a second; yours never glow.
  • Watch again. A missed turn plays back in about ten seconds: route, reasons, reads, changes, page frames, with Undo just this.
  • Asks that wait for the internet. Offline asks wait as "when online" chips and run on return if under an hour old; retries are counted on the line.

Understanding

  • Why? on anything. A "?" on hover over a row, a box, a chip or the line, answered from records first and the agent last.
  • What's DNS? A shipped book of about forty ideas, each a picture filled in with this machine's values and history.
  • The chain of why. A failure as at most four boxes pinned to log lines; guesses dashed and marked "probably".
  • A text twin for every picture. For copying, captions and screen readers; reading aloud belongs with the speech service planned for a later milestone (see Bombadil's voice).
  • One motion and sound language. Out of the pill and back into it; orange glow for the machine's changes; undo plays backwards; sounds off by default.

What it knows

  • A fresh page you can see. A new session waits for a quiet moment, carries a visible bundle, and marks the welcome line; cut-off work returns as a chip.
  • A small local model. Opt-in on capable hardware, reached only from the offline line, for small asks.

The machine

  • Laptop basics. Keys with a level in the pill, the clock card, and a lid that stays awake on power while work runs.
  • Plugging something in. "SanDisk USB drive, 64 GB, 12 GB used. [Open] [Eject]"; phones and headphones explain themselves.
  • Several screens. Each screen its own pill; things open where you asked from; layouts remembered per monitor.
  • Mic, camera and screen marks. Coloured by who is using them, with switches that cut the device for everyone.
  • Why is it slow? Answered locally with bars by who; extends the desk's Machine card.
  • This computer, and next to Windows. What works and what needs a driver before install; the disk drawn as a bar with Windows, free space and Bombadil's slice.
  • Print and scan. Jobs with a page-counting chip; scans the brain knows came from the scanner.

Apps, space and change

  • Ordinary Linux apps. Repositories first, then Flathub, then the AUR through the recipe check; open by name.
  • Where the disk went. Restore points, downloaded updates and runtimes always listed, with how far undo reaches.
  • What's different from stock. One list in the Brain's System area, each row with who, why and Put back.
  • The machine as a recipe. Everything on top of stock with its reasons; "set this up like my old laptop" redoes it on new hardware.

Trust mine, not checked

  • Lock and encryption. Full-disk encryption on by default at install, hyprlock on lid and idle, notifications without content while locked.
  • A keyring from the first login. So Chromium, gh and the CLIs store secrets properly instead of under a fixed fallback key.
  • Who talks to the internet. Connections and bytes by the machine, sessions and apps, coloured like everything else.
  • A clipboard that forgets passwords. History skips anything marked sensitive and clears after a while.

Beyond this machine mine, not checked

  • The phone. Promises and "something broke" reach the phone when you are away; the brain brief's "brain from the phone" gap.
  • A second Bombadil. Keeping memory, routines and chosen folders in step is not designed.
  • Mail, calendar and people. Starts with a guest app such as Thunderbird.

OS basics mine, not checked

  • Languages, keyboards and time. "add a Greek keyboard", "answer me in Portuguese"; the clock follows you when you travel.
  • Notifications from other apps. The decided one pill line, filed in history, never a toast.
  • One theme for guests too. Dark and light reach Chromium, GTK and Qt apps through the portal's appearance setting.

Owned by other pieces of work

  • The Bombadil Desk. Now draws the route piece 1 turns on; Watching shows piece 4's jobs; Away shows routine runs and the night's upkeep; the Machine card is where "What broke it" appears.
  • Bombadil's voice, and welcome and empty states. The words of the welcome line belong to that piece of work; this brief only adds what it counts.
  • The self-improvement loop. Repeated asks turned into widgets, apps or routines; Bombadil finding and fixing its own bugs; growing the kit. A routine it proposes lands in Autopilot.

Cut, and why

  • Updates installed at night on their own. The machine would start work nobody asked for, and a background install beside a turn breaks one turn, one restore point. Downloading at night stays.
  • A route strip under the line. The desk's Now owns the route.
  • A while-you-were-away card of its own. The desk's Away is that card.
  • A second model narrating the turn. Slower every turn, and two narrators disagree.
  • Pictures of the machine written by the model. A passenger cannot check them.
  • Free force-layout diagrams. Unreadable past a dozen boxes, different every time.
  • An "Explain more?" offer learned from repeated presses. Learning from repeated asks belongs to the self-improvement loop.
  • A local model installed by default. Gigabytes on every install for a feature most days never use.
  • Mounting drives only on a click. A small permission prompt by another name.

Gaps worth thinking about
