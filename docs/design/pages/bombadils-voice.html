Bombadil's Voice

You sign in, and a small card asks what to call you and how Bombadil should sound. You type Alex and press Enter. From then on every boot opens with one line, Good morning, Alex. Where to today?, that fades once you touch the keyboard, and every empty place says what is true and offers one way forward.

Voice here means how Bombadil writes: its greetings, goodbyes and the last sentence of each reply. Speaking aloud belongs with the speech service planned for later. The logo and the visual identity belong to their own piece of work.

Build first · piece 1

The setup card

Right after you sign in, one small card rises above the pill. It asks for your name and offers three voices, and each voice shows how it would greet you, with your name already in it. Try it: type a name, use Up and Down, press Enter, or press Esc to skip.

Signed in to Claude. What should I call you?
Enter keeps Merry · Up and Down to change · Esc skips
Ask anything10:12
How long
About ten seconds. No settings page and no model call.
Enter or Esc
Enter keeps the highlighted voice. Esc skips with the defaults: Merry and no name. The defaults are saved the moment the card appears, so it is never asked again.
Why a field
The name goes in the card's own field, so it can never be taken for a request. Typed into the pill, install docker would rename you Install Docker.
No guessing
The card starts empty. The installer's account is called user and the sign-in carries only an email address, so a guessed name would often be wrong every morning.
Later
call me Lex renames you, be less chatty moves you to Plain, and the one word voice brings the card back filled in.
Where it lives
~/.config/bombadil/persona.toml, three keys: name, voice, greet.

Three voices

Try the voices

Three voices that differ on one thing each: Merry and Plain differ in style, Quiet in how often it speaks. Pick one and every line on this page changes, the card above and the day below included. The facts in a line never change with the voice.

What every voice keeps the same: the facts, the marks, the working line, On it, Undo and Details, provider trouble, limits, sign-out, offline and the put-back line. A voice owns three things only: greetings and goodbyes, the invitation, and the model's own last sentence.

A day with the voice

Arrivals and departures, nothing in between

Bombadil speaks when you arrive or leave and is silent at rest. Each moment below is drawn in the voice and with the name you picked above.

    The format

    The welcome line

    One row, 100 characters at most: a hello, and at most one sentence of facts. Every part is built on the machine from what it knows, so it is instant, free and works offline at boot.

    OpenerGood morning
    ,
    Name, if savedAlex
    .
    Body214 updates are waiting, 4 of them security fixes.

    Opener, by moment

    • Welcome on the first boot ever, and whenever the time zone is not set yet.
    • Good morning, afternoon, evening on the first boot of a day, only with a real time zone.
    • Late one between midnight and five, Merry only, once.
    • Welcome back after four hours, overnight, or three days away.
    • No opener after 30 minutes to 4 hours away: only the facts.
    • Good night only ever as a goodbye at shutdown. Nothing tells anyone to go to bed.

    Body: the top one or two true things

    1. Something failed or was put back, and you have not seen it.
    2. Something waits on you, unless the pill already says so.
    3. Something finished.
    4. Something was stopped by a restart.
    5. What you made, only after three days away.
    6. Updates waiting, with the security count.
    7. A promise that came true.

    With no facts, Merry adds one invitation, Plain adds nothing, and Quiet says nothing at all. Each fact is said once and remembered in a small ledger.

    Where and how long

    • The line above the pill, a second after the bar is up.
    • It waits for your first key or mouse movement, then fades 8 seconds later (15 for the first hello), and is gone after 30 seconds at most. Hover holds it.
    • Never the pill's placeholder, never a card, never sticky. Dropped, not queued, behind a full-screen window.

    What wins the line

    Setup›A running turn›Errors and limits›Your turn›The greeting

    The greeting is dropped whenever anything else holds the line. A cheerful Good morning over an offline line would be false.

    Merry's invitations

    Chosen by the day count since setup, never at random. The project owner's own line, Let's go for a walk!, comes on day 0 and every fourth day; the other seven take turns on the days between, so none of them comes back within nine days.

    day 0Let's go for a walk!

    day 8Let's go for a walk!

    day 11Shall we wander somewhere?

    Build these first

    Two pieces

    Both edit the pill's line and the shell, so they follow the native sign-in change (the setup channel, and the installer's copy of ~/.config/bombadil, both on main) and fit around the coding sessions' placeholder change.

    1

    Bombadil says hello

    Effort S to M, about two days

    What you see: the card after sign-in, your name and a voice in about ten seconds, then the chosen voice's first line. From then on every boot opens with one line in that voice, replies from either AI sound the same way, and call me Lex, be less chatty and voice change it.

    Why first: it is the widget the project owner asked for and the line the owner wrote. Everything else only fills that line with more true things.

    • persona.py reads and writes persona.toml (name, voice, greet), defensively, so a broken file can never stop agentd.
    • One sentence in the system prompt for both providers, 22 to 42 words, never in coding sessions and never added to every turn.
    • PersonaCard.qml joins the bar like the setup chips and takes the keyboard through the non-toggling summon.
    • A welcome mode for the line, and one greeting per login that a restart of agentd never replays.
    • The word voice in the launcher, and a plain step with no Undo when the file changes.
    • Tests: the name check, the card offscreen, both providers carrying the sentence, and every line in every voice fitting 100 characters.

    Before building: check in the VM that a resumed session picks up a changed voice, and that the card really gets the keyboard when the browser leaves.

    2

    What it knows, and every empty place

    Effort M

    What you see: the boot line starts carrying news, a return says what happened while you were away, a shutdown says what will wait for you, and every empty place says what is true with one next step.

    Why second: its sources land in other pieces of work (the session registry, the update count, an away signal). Each is one small function that returns a clause or nothing, so the line improves as they land.

    • greet.py, pure: the grammar, the ranking and the ledger, with every voice's words in one shipped table.
    • turns.jsonl rows gain made and changed, plus one boot row, so since last time has an anchor.
    • Away from Quickshell's idle monitor; until it exists only boot and goodbye are live.
    • The goodbye is sent as the shutdown's start text, because the done text may never be drawn.
    • EmptyState in the app kit gains one ask chip, and the shell's own empty answers use the format.
    • The clock: the installer stops forcing UTC and time sync is turned on. Until a zone is set there are no time-of-day words, and the first hello offers Set my time zone.

    What holds it together

    The rules

    Each one exists to stop the voice turning into a mascot: the same line every morning, a joke next to a failure, a cheerful hello over an error, a name in every sentence.

    1. Speaks when you arrive or leave, silent at rest.A greeting fades by itself. The rest screen stays wallpaper and a pill, and closing the last window shows nothing.
    2. Every line is true.Built from the clock, the turn log and the registries. A fact is said once, and a line with nothing true to say is silence.
    3. The voice changes words, never facts.Counts, names, marks and errors read the same in every voice, because people search for them and paste them into bug reports.
    4. One line, never a chat.A hello and at most one sentence of facts, 100 characters. A voice may not lengthen the decided limits.
    5. Nothing starts without you.A line offers and never runs. A chip is a sentence you could have typed.
    6. Changing it is a sentence, not a page.“Call me Lex”, “be less chatty” and the word “voice” are the whole settings.
    7. It costs nothing at boot.Greetings are local templates: instant, free, offline. The model writes only its own last sentence, inside a turn you asked for.
    8. An empty place gives one way to fill it.It names the place, says why, offers one real doorway, and never says “Oops” or a bare “Nothing here yet”.

    Defaults

    Decisions I picked a default for

    Each of these forks. I picked a default and say why. The first three are the questions at the top of the page.

    How should the one-time setup look?one card above the pill after sign-in: a name field and three voices with live samples.›

    It is the widget the project owner described, the name goes in a field that cannot be mistaken for an ask, and a voice is judged by a sample, not a name.

    Other options questions in the pill (a typed name can be mistaken for a request); name only (the voice is what the owner asked for).

    Where does the welcome show, and for how long?the line above the pill, fading eight seconds after your first touch.›

    It is the one voice already, fades by itself and leaves the rest screen as accepted.

    Other options a big hello on the wallpaper for the first boot only (memorable, but a new element); a card until clicked (a popup by another name).

    How far does the voice reach?greetings, goodbyes and the model's own last sentence.›

    The working line, receipts, errors, limits and empty places stay plain in every voice.

    Other options greetings only (the character vanishes when you ask for anything); everything including the working line (the line stops being a plain record).

    Does Bombadil ask what the machine is for?no.›

    The tour already ends with “Make me something for what I do”, which asks when the answer pays off, and nothing uses the answer on day one.

    Other options one optional tap on the card, about three seconds more.

    Does Bombadil ask what to call itself?no.›

    The OS is called Bombadil and says “I” or nothing. A renamed agent would touch every error line and doc for no gain.

    Does the card suggest your name?no, it starts empty.›

    The account is called user with no full name, the sign-in status prints no name, and the email's first part is often not a first name. A wrong name every morning is worse than a blank field.

    Other options prefill from the email as a suggestion, if a real login shows it carries a first name.

    How many voices?three: Merry, Plain, Quiet.›

    A warm reassuring voice is out, because it would tell the model to say what is safe, which it cannot check.

    Other options four, with a dry deadpan one (a joke next to a failure is hard to forbid by rule); two, a chatty or quiet switch.

    Who writes the lines?local templates, no model call.›

    A model-written greeting adds seconds and tokens to every boot, fails offline, is an uninvited turn and drifts off the voice.

    Where do name and voice live?~/.config/bombadil/persona.toml.›

    config.toml is rewritten whole on every sign-in, and memory.md is loaded by every coding session, where a merry voice would leak into code reviews.

    Other options a line in memory.md.

    How does the model learn the voice?one sentence in the system prompt, for both providers.›

    One sandbox test kept the old sentence on a resumed session, which is a VM check before building.

    Other options a block before every turn (reaches resumed sessions, but about 90 words per turn pile up).

    How do you change it?by asking, and the one word “voice”.›

    The agent edits a two-key file with its ordinary tools and agentd checks it on load. “Be less chatty” picks the voice; “explain less” is the separate explain level.

    Other options a settings page; sliders for warmth and humour.

    What does a greeting say after a short break?only the facts, no hello, no name, and nothing when nothing happened.›

    Greeting someone after every coffee makes the name a tic by the fifth time.

    A goodbye on restart?none.›

    A restart is how a pending undo is applied, and “Restarting.” with its undo text is what you need to read.

    What if nobody answers the card?the defaults are already saved, and it is never asked again.›

    A question that returns every boot is a dialog by another name.

    Do chips ride on the greeting?no.›

    The example chips and the habit chips stay as the first brief decided. The one exception is “Set my time zone” on a machine still on UTC.

    Speech?writing only, for now.›

    A greeting that speaks, or a chime, belongs with the speech service planned for a later milestone.

    Left out

    Cut, and why

    • A setting for what Bombadil calls itself. The name is the product.
    • A question about what the machine is for. Nothing uses the answer on day one.
    • Sliders, or a long list of tones. Named voices are chosen in one glance, and sliders invite tuning instead of choosing.
    • Warm, Dry and Steward voices. Warm reassures without receipts, Dry's jokes are hard to keep off failures, Steward is Plain with longer words.
    • A model-written greeting. Slow, costly, and broken offline at boot.
    • Guessing the name from the account, email, git config or unix user. Checked: empty or wrong.
    • A standing welcome, splash, clock or weather at rest. The rest state is wallpaper and a pill.
    • Unasked lines: evening recaps, idle nudges, tips, streaks, “day 30 with Bombadil”. The agent never starts on its own, and Time is the one place for what happened.
    • “Today you installed Docker” in a goodnight. You know what you did.
    • A voice on the working line, receipts, errors or limits. They are facts and safety marks.
    • A voice in coding sessions or memory.md. A session is a program you run, not a second voice of the machine.
    • A greeting on every unlock, on the lock screen, or at restart. Filler, or hidden behind the lock.
    • Custom voices such as “talk like a pirate”. The local lines could not follow, so the character would vanish at every boot.
    • Asking for the name again after a skip. Nagging.

    Earlier briefs

    What this changes in the earlier designs

    Everything decided before stays in force. These are the places this design touches.

    • How Bombadil should feel, first boot: “Signed in. Ask me for anything.” becomes the card and the chosen first line, on the first sign-in only. The tour, the Install chip, the example chips and the faint line keep their order.
    • How Bombadil should feel, a week later: the welcome-back line keeps its decided words (Plain reproduces them) and gains a name and a voice.
    • What skips the model: gains one word, “voice”. Shutdown gains a goodbye; restart is unchanged.
    • Building on Bombadil: “While you were away” and the your-turn line are the same words. The greeting never repeats what the pill says, and sessions get no voice.
    • The Bombadil Desk: the Away card is the list behind the greeting; while it is up the line shrinks to its opener and a count. No widget gets an empty state.
    • Bombadil's Brain: Focus, the Map and Time use the empty-place format and say how long the Brain has been watching.
    • Riding with Bombadil: its update and security counts and its promises that came true are fact clauses here. Its explain level stays separate from the voice.
    • App kit: EmptyState gains an ask chip; its old defaults stay as fallbacks.
    • Native sign-in: the card rides its setup channel and its installer copy of ~/.config/bombadil.
    • Installer: stops forcing UTC over a zone chosen on the live USB.

    Checked today

    Checked on 29 and 30 Sep 2026 against main and the open branches, partly by running commands in a scratch copy of the installed system.

    • The installer forces the clock to UTC inside the installed system and creates the account as user with no full name, so time-of-day words would be wrong for most people until a zone is set.
    • No name to prefill: the Claude sign-in status prints no name, and the saved account holds only an id and an email address.
    • The installer wipes the live home, so nothing carries into the install by itself. The native sign-in change copies ~/.config/bombadil back, which is the only reason the card's answer survives install.
    • Restore points cover the system, not /home yet, so undoing a rename waits for the planned /home config.
    • The system prompt reaches both providers on fresh and resumed turns. In one sandbox test a resumed Claude session kept an old appended sentence, so that is the first VM check.
    • The pill's line has five modes (idle, working, closing, local, setup); the welcome mode would be a sixth, and the card uses the non-toggling summon an earlier change added.
    • The shutdown text you see is the start text, sent before the power-off; the done text may never be drawn, so the goodbye goes in the start text.
    • Nothing in the system reports that you are away yet: hypridle is not installed. Quickshell's idle monitor can.
    • turns.jsonl has no made or changed field and no boot row; piece 2 adds them.

    To verify in the VM before building

    1. A resumed session on both providers picks up a changed voice.
    2. The card takes the keyboard when the browser panel slides away, and typing in it never reaches the pill.
    3. Quickshell's idle monitor reports idle under Hyprland.
    4. The goodbye is drawn before the power goes.
    5. The clock follows a zone set with timedatectl without restarting the shell.

    Open questions

    • Who sets the time zone on a live USB with no network? The design never states a wrong hour, but someone really in UTC looks the same as an unset machine.
    • Should the explain level (Brief, Normal, Teach) live beside the voice, and should Quiet imply Brief? Today they are separate.
    • The launcher word “voice” may sit next to push-to-talk later; worth a second look when speech ships.
    • Which screen greets on several monitors follows the first brief's open question about the pill's screen.
