The Bombadil Desk

You type install docker and set it up for my projects and press Enter. The line above the pill says On it, and a small card rises in the bottom-left corner with your words as its title and the route under them: four steps, the first ticked, the second pulsing orange with the exact command in amber, the last two grey. Beside it, Watching counts down a download and a timer. In the bottom-right corner, Needs you lists the two coding sessions waiting for you, one of them asking for root, and one tap gives it; above it, Machine says builder is near its memory limit, in blue. Then you open the browser. It slides in over the rails, and every card folds into a chip beside the pill, so nothing is lost and nothing is in the way. Close the browser and the cards come back where they were. One word and one gesture to learn: desk folds everything to chips and back, and a drag by the title moves a card.

This is the widget design for Bombadil's desktop. It sits on top of the three experience briefs of 27 Sep 2026 (How Bombadil should feel, Building on Bombadil and Bombadil's Brain), whose 29, 19 and 17 decisions stay in force. The project owner asked for widgets that are modular and that move, hide and make room for the apps, popups and other visuals the AI brings, on a machine where the AI drives and the person is a passenger who gets things explained visually. The desk is how widgets live with the pill and the wallpaper; it does not re-argue the rest state, and every decision in the three briefs was checked against this design (the Cut section says what that took out). The owner accepted the design and all fifteen defaults on 28 Sep 2026, so they are confirmed decisions, and the build of the first two pieces starts from here.

The rules

  1. Every widget answers one question a passenger asks.What is it doing, and why? What is it keeping an eye on? What is waiting for me? Who is working? What happened while I was gone? Is the machine in trouble? And, only when asked for, what is being touched right now? A widget that answers none of these is not built.
  2. The middle belongs to the answer; the margins belong to the passenger.Windows, panels and cards land where the earlier briefs put them. Widgets live in two rails at the sides and in the pill's row. A widget never sits under anything: whatever the AI brings has right of way, and the widget folds toward its edge and comes back when the thing leaves.
  3. Three faces, and the small ones already exist.A full face (a card in a rail), a strip (a chip beside the pill: the session dots, the job chips and the your-turn line the earlier briefs decided) and a dot (a mark on the pill). The desk always shows the largest face that fits.
  4. Present only with something to say.No plan, no Now; nothing counting, no Watching; one session waiting is the line, not a card. A machine at rest is wallpaper and a pill, exactly as decided, with no vitals strip. The desk fills exactly as the machine gets busy, which is itself the explanation.
  5. Widgets show what the line cannot.The line above the pill stays the one voice: the verb, the seconds, the closing sentence and Undo live there and nowhere else. A widget adds the route, the list, the meter, the who.
  6. Colour says who.White for you, orange for the machine's turn, blue for coding sessions, the brain's three colours, on every meter, dot and strip, so a glance says who is using the memory or who touched the file.
  7. No click starts work you did not ask for, and nothing pops.Do it and Open send your answer on as a turn with its restore point or bring a session forward; Undo, Stop and Why? never touch the model. No widget takes the keyboard, no widget interrupts, and the agent rearranges the desk only when you ask. Built-in widgets are shell QML; a widget the agent makes publishes typed JSON the shell draws with the same faces, the rule the answer cards already follow.

The desk

Four regions. The stage in the middle is where windows, panels and cards land, exactly as decided. Two rails run down the sides; the row is the pill's own row; the column above the pill is the conversation's. Widgets use the rails and the row and never the stage or the column.

Which railLeft, bottom up: Now, Watching, Alive. Right, bottom up: Needs you, Away, Machine. Nearest the pill is most urgent. Because an absent widget takes no slot, the stacks compact: with no turn running, Watching sits at the bottom left.
What fitsEach rail holds what fits. From the bottom up, a widget takes its full face if its height fits in the room left; it and everything above it fold to strips otherwise. On 1920×1080 everything fits; on a 1280×720 laptop two per rail fit and a third is a strip. Three strips per side at most; beyond that they merge into one counting chip.
Right of wayWhatever the AI brings covers the rails freely; nothing reserves space, so windows land exactly where the first brief put them. A widget whose slot is covered, even partly, folds within 150 ms into its strip and unfolds 400 ms after the cover leaves, widget by widget, so a window dragged past does not flicker the rail.
ModesThe desk follows the pill's three decided modes. Open desk: full faces. Shared desk, an app or panel in front: the pill narrows to 360px, covered widgets fold, the rest stay, and the compositor's own dim under a special workspace makes the app the thing in front. Immersive, full-screen video or a game: the capsule pill only, the desk hidden, the mark on the dot the only trace. desk folds every face to its strip and back.
Peek and openHovering a strip raises its full face above the row for as long as you hover; clicking keeps it up until you click elsewhere (Esc closes it only while the pill has the keyboard), and it never takes the keyboard. Clicking a full face's title opens the thing behind it: the turn's details, Time, the Map.
Drawn by whomTwo new Quickshell windows, one per rail, on the Bottom layer (above the wallpaper, below every window), reserving nothing, with an input mask. Coverage comes from Hyprland's window list and events plus the bar's own cards, refreshed every 500 ms while a window is on the stage, since Hyprland sends no event during a drag. The kit's Theme is imported, not copied. One DeskState object, driven by agentd's events like PillState, tested offscreen the same way.

The widgets

Seven, each in the kit's look: a 300px card with a title, one line of why, and rows built from Panel, ListRow, Meter, Sparkline and Badge. Each is shown here at its real size with its strip and its dot.

1

Now

What is it doing, and why?

When a turn states a plan of two or more steps, or touches the system, a card rises in the bottom-left rail with your ask as its title and the route under it: a tick for each done step, the current one pulsing orange with the step's own words, the next ones grey. A system step gets the amber edge and the exact command; one no restore point can undo gets the red edge and, while it runs, the words can't be undone · Esc stops it. The line of why counts what the turn has touched. A turn a session asked for is titled install qemu-full · asked by builder. When the turn ends every step ticks and the card leaves with the line. Nothing the line already says is repeated here: no verb, no seconds, no closing sentence, no Undo.

Appears
With a plan of two or more steps, or the first system step; leaves with the line.
Feeds
agentd keeps the turn's task table from the CLI's task tools (Claude Code's TaskCreate and TaskUpdate in stream-json, TodoWrite on older versions; Codex's todo list, whose current step is the first unfinished one) and broadcasts it as a plan event stamped with the turn; turn_start gains asked_by.
Kit
Panel, ListRow, Badge, Mono, the StatusLine's edge colours.
2

Watching

What is it keeping an eye on?

One row per thing counting right now: a Meter for a job with progress, a countdown for a timer, a plain row for a one-shot watcher on something running, each with a small × that stops it. A finished job turns green and stays 12 seconds; a failed one turns red with its last line of output under its name and Why?, which opens the rest in the terminal panel. A card still counting when a newer card replaces it moves in here instead of dying. Standing watchers (the morning kind) are not rows; what are you watching? lists them, as decided.

Appears
With the first thing counting; leaves when nothing is.
Feeds
A jobs registry in agentd: a job file per systemd-run --user unit a turn starts, the timer cards, one-shot watchers. Stop runs systemctl --user stop, never the model.
Kit
Panel, ListRow, Meter, IconButton, Badge.
3

Needs you

What is waiting for me?

The coding sessions' asks, one row each with its own button: a session whose turn it is (Open) and a session asking the machine for something it may not do itself (Do it). The rows are the second brief's Tab walk shown as rows, and Tab from the empty pill still walks them. One session waiting is the your-turn line, as decided, and no card; the card rises when two or more wait. The agent's own question, notifications and provider lines stay in the pill and never move to the desk. It cannot be hidden; it is silent when nothing waits.

Appears
When two or more sessions wait; leaves when the walk is empty.
Feeds
dev.py's your-turn and request events from the managed hooks (second brief, pieces 2 and 3).
Kit
Panel, ListRow, Badge, Button.
4

Sessions

Who is working?

The project chips beside the pill with a dot per session (moving while it works, lit when it is your turn, red when it failed, dim when asleep) are the widget; hovering a dot peeks its session, as decided. Its full face is the what's running? card, one row per session grouped by project with name, state, last plain line, files changed, PR state and the check's result. It opens on asking or a click on the chip and leaves like any card; it never stands in a rail, because a permanent sessions sidebar is the IDE the second brief refused.

Appears
The chip with the first session; the card on asking. After a reboot the dots come back dim.
Feeds
The dev registry, the hooks, checkpoint refs and check results (second brief, pieces 1, 2 and 6).
Kit
Panel, ListRow, Badge, Caption.
5

Away

What happened while I was gone?

When you come back after a break, the while you were away group the first two briefs decided, as a card in the right rail instead of only a line: what finished, who is waiting, what failed, and what changed with Undo just this for turns older than the one on the line. Click a row and the Brain opens in Time at that moment. It leaves once you have seen it: a click, or your next ask.

Appears
When you come back; leaves once seen.
Feeds
turns.jsonl, the snapper pairs, the dev and jobs registries, grouped locally as decided; the brain's timeline when it lands.
Kit
Panel, ListRow, LineButton.
6

Machine

Is the machine in trouble?

Nothing, most of the day. When something crosses a line the briefs already speak about (memory near the sessions' limit, the disk nearly full, the processor hot) a card rises with one line of why and the meters stacked by who is using them, plus the network rate. Clicking the disk meter opens the Map in size mode. How's the machine? opens the same face on request. It leaves when everything is back under its line. Battery, brightness and volume stay in the pill and the status card, as decided.

Appears
While something is over its line, or on asking.
Feeds
A 1 Hz sampler in agentd: /proc, the cgroup files of turn scopes, the dev slice and app units, thermal zones, NetworkManager.
Kit
Meter, StackedBar, Sparkline, Stat.
7

Alive

What is being touched right now? (opt in)

A small corner of the Brain's Map: the twenty or so most alive things, each pulsing in the actor's colour when it is touched, with the current turn's files lit and named. It is on the desk only after show alive; while on, it is present while anything pulsed in the last minute. Click a dot: Focus on it. Comes after brain piece 1, when there is something to draw.

Appears
Only after show alive, then while anything pulsed in the last minute.
Feeds
bombadil-brain's subscribe stream.
Kit
The Map's scene item.

Making room

The same afternoon, four things the AI brings. Nothing reserves space; the widget yields, and comes back.

Move, hide, show

Drag a full face by its title to the other rail, to another rank, or into the row to fold it by hand; drag a strip into a rail to give it a slot. Or say it with the launcher's own words: widget names join the name list, so hide machine and show alive are the verbs that already put a window away and bring one up, and desk folds everything to strips and back. A sentence (put watching on the right) goes to the agent. Positions persist in ~/.local/state/bombadil/desk.toml, outside the restore points as that directory already is, so an undo never moves your desk.

deskhide machineshow aliveshow sessions

The agent gets one os-mcp tool, desk (show, hide, move, make, remove). It works only in a turn whose words asked for the desk (show, keep, pin, hide, put, make a widget) and refuses otherwise, and the line says what it did. A widget that appears unasked would be a popup by another name.

A widget made on request

Show my batch on the desk or make a widget that shows my Tracker streak makes an eighth widget. It is an app in ~/Apps with a [widget] section in app.toml, and its app.py publishes its state through Widget.set({...}) in the same typed face kinds the built-in widgets use (steps, rows, meters, timeline, stat, text). It gets hot reload, persisted state and the per-app git undo like any app. A pin still turns a card into an app, as decided; keep this on the desk makes that app also a widget. If its app stops publishing, the face dims and says stale · 3 min, and nothing an app publishes can block the shell. A free QML face, drawn by the app's own process and placed on the rail through layer-shell, comes later, never inside the shell.

A day at the desk

  1. 09:05

    You sit down. Wallpaper, the pill, a Bombadil chip with three dim dots, and one card: While you were away: batch finished, 300 done; reviewer is waiting; tidy my downloads, Undo. You click Open on reviewer's row and the card is gone.

  2. 10:30, a long install

    install docker and set it up for my projects. Now rises with the route, four steps. The second goes amber with its command; you read it, you do not have to answer it. At 58 seconds every step ticks, the closing line says Installed Docker. with Undo, and the card leaves with the line.

  3. 11:15, two sessions want you

    The line says builder asks: install qemu-full, and reviewer's turn comes at the same moment, so Needs you rises with both rows. You tap Do it. The install is an ordinary turn in Now, titled install qemu-full · asked by builder, amber, with a restore point, and the row leaves.

  4. 12:00, the browser

    The panel slides in over both rails, and four cards become four chips beside the narrow pill. You hover 2 need you to peek, close the browser, and the cards are back before you notice the fold.

  5. 14:30, a batch

    show my batch on the desk. A Batch widget rises above Now with a meter, 14 of 20, blue, and the line says why it is there.

  6. 15:10, memory

    builder passes the sessions' memory limit. Machine rises on the right with builder is near its memory limit and a meter that is mostly blue; you know what to end, and the card leaves when it is back under the line.

  7. 18:30

    desk. Every card folds to a chip beside the pill: wallpaper, the pill and a row of chips. desk again and they are back.

Build these first

Two pieces that can each be built on their own. Both edit shell.qml, so they follow the merges of the app kit and of the session dots (which are Sessions' strip). Where each piece stands on 2026-10-01 is in the brief.

1

The desk, and Now

M to L

What changes: two rail windows on the Bottom layer with exclusionMode: Normal, exclusiveZone: 0 and input masks; the row laid out as strips, pill, strips; DeskState.qml (faces, slots, what fits, fold and unfold from Hyprland coverage with the 150/400 ms hysteresis, the three modes, persistence in desk.toml); the fold animation; the 500 ms client refresh while a window is up; widget names in launcher.py's name list and the word desk; the desk tool in os-mcp, gated on the turn's words; the typed face kinds as shell components built from the kit's Theme, imported through QML_IMPORT_PATH. Now: agentd keeps the per-turn task table from TaskCreate/TaskUpdate and Codex's todo list and broadcasts it as plan events with the turn id, adds asked_by to turn_start and the touched counts to its status events; the route face reads plan, status, risk, command and turn_end, and the red step's caption.

Before building: check in the VM that a Bottom-layer window with exclusiveZone: 0 draws above the wallpaper and below floating and special-workspace windows on Hyprland 0.56 and reserves nothing; that a 500 ms refresh of Quickshell's window rectangles keeps up with a dragged window without costing the shell; how far Hyprland's dim_special reaches; and that a press in a rail window ending over the bar window can be resolved from screen coordinates.

2

Needs you, and Watching

M

What changes: the rows face for both; Needs you reads dev.py's your-turn and request events and owns the Tab walk the second brief specified, so there is one queue, with the card only above one waiting session; Watching gets the jobs registry in agentd (a job file per systemd-run --user unit a turn starts, the timer cards, one-shot watchers) and the chips the first brief decided become its strip; a counting card survives replacement by moving here. The Stop × runs systemctl --user stop on the unit, never the model.

Then: Away (turns.jsonl and the registries now, the brain's timeline when it lands), Machine (the sampler with cgroup attribution and its lines), Sessions' card as the chip's peek (after the session dots), Alive (after brain piece 1), and widgets the agent makes (the [widget] section, Widget.set, keep this on the desk).

Decisions, confirmed by the owner on 28 Sep 2026

Each of these forks. I picked a default and say why; the alternative is listed for the record. The owner confirmed all fifteen on 28 Sep 2026.

Where do widgets live?two rails at the sides, 300px wide, stacked from the bottom.›

The stage keeps the middle and the column keeps the conversation, so the answer lands where the first brief put it and the eye moves sideways from the line to the route.

Other options a grid of tiles on the wallpaper (fights the stage for the middle); a single dock along the bottom (the row already is that, for strips); one corner only.

Do widgets make room, or do windows avoid them?widgets yield; nothing reserves space.›

The answer is the thing itself and must land where it lands; a rail with an exclusive zone would shrink every window and push the browser off centre.

Other options rails reserve space and windows centre in what is left; windows avoid rails only when they fit.

Are widgets always on?present only with something to say, and nothing at rest, not even a vitals strip.›

This keeps the confirmed rest state and turns the desk itself into the explanation: a busy machine has a busy desk, a quiet one is wallpaper and a pill.

Other options always on; a strip of vitals at rest; a switch per widget.

How many sizes?three faces, where the strip and the dot are the chips and marks already decided.›

One concept, not two: the session dots, the job chips, the your-turn line and the mark on the dot are the small faces of these widgets.

Other options one size that hides; freely resizable cards.

What when the rail is short?what fits gets a full face, the rest fold to strips, and strips beyond three per side merge into one counting chip.›

The second brief already folds projects into one chip above three; the same fold covers everything.

Other options scrolling rails; shrinking every face.

Which rail for what?left the machine's (Now, Watching, Alive), right yours (Needs you, Away, Machine), nearest the pill most urgent.›

Needs you sits by the clock and the dot's mark; Now sits beside the line.

Other options one rail; ordered by age.

Where does the route go?Now in the left rail, showing only the route, never what the line says.›

The line stays one line and the column stays the conversation's, as decided; the route is a widget, so it folds like one, and it appears only when there is a route to show.

Other options the line grows into the route while a turn runs; a card for every turn.

How fast does it fold and come back?fold within 150 ms, unfold 400 ms after the cover leaves, and a partial cover counts, widget by widget.›

Fast out of the way, calm coming back, no flicker while a window is dragged past; a window over the top of a rail leaves the widget by the pill alone.

Other options symmetric 200 ms; fold only when fully covered; fold the whole rail when any of it is touched.

Who draws widgets?the shell, from typed data, for built-in and agent-made widgets alike; free QML in the widget's own process later.›

The cards' rule, kept: nothing the agent writes runs inside the one way to talk to the machine.

Other options every widget its own process from the start (a Bottom-layer window from PySide6 needs layer-shell-qt, which the kit does not have yet); widget QML loaded into Quickshell.

Can the agent rearrange the desk?only when asked; the desk tool refuses in a turn whose words did not ask for the desk.›

The first brief's agent never starts work on its own, and a widget that appears unasked is a popup by another name.

Other options the agent curates the desk freely; the agent may add a widget when it explains why; never.

Peek or open?hover a strip to peek, click to keep the face up until you click away, never take the keyboard.›

The second brief's hover-peek on a dot, generalised to every strip.

Other options click to unfold the whole rail; no peek.

Can everything be hidden?everything but Needs you.›

It is the only place two sessions' requests can be answered together, and it is silent when empty.

Other options everything; nothing.

Which colours?the brain's three (white you, orange the machine, blue sessions) on every meter and dot, and the line's amber and red for system and irreversible steps.›

Three colours can be read at a glance and already mean who; a colour per widget would mean nothing.

Other options a colour per widget; per resource.

One screen or all?the pill's screen only.›

Parallel sessions are where a second screen matters, and which screen the pill owns is still the first brief's open question; the desk follows that answer.

Other option rails on every screen.

Does the desk exist on the live USB?yes, all of it.›

Away says undo starts after install, as the pill already does.

Other option strips only until installed.

Cut, and why

  • Today, a card of the day's receipts. The brain brief folded Rewind into Time so there is one place for what happened; a second history in a rail would disagree with it by afternoon. Away keeps the one moment a list is wanted.
  • A vitals strip at rest. The rest state is wallpaper and a pill, decided; a number with nothing wrong is a gauge, and the battery is in the pill.
  • A standing Sessions card. The second brief names a permanent sessions sidebar as the IDE failure mode; the dots are the widget, the card is a peek.
  • A notification centre. Needs you holds only the sessions' asks; the agent's question, notifications and provider lines stay in the pill as decided.
  • Undo on a widget. Three Undo buttons for one turn is a puzzle; Undo stays on the line, and only Away offers Undo just this for older turns.
  • A clock, weather, calendar or notes widget. Any desktop has them, none answers a passenger's question about this machine, and the clock is in the pill.
  • A dock or app icons on the wallpaper. The pill is the launcher, and typing a name is faster than finding an icon.
  • A chat or transcript widget. The one way to turn the desktop back into a chat window.
  • A token or cost counter. Internals on screen; the limit line in the pill covers the moment it matters.
  • Resizing widgets by hand, or a grid you arrange freely. Three faces are the sizes, and positions that drift are positions you cannot learn; slots in a rail hold still, like the Map's areas.
  • New words for the desk. widgets, clear the desk and put X on the right would be five forms to learn; desk and the launcher's own show and hide are enough, and a sentence goes to the agent.
  • Rails on the lock screen. The agent has sudo; the desk shows what it did, and that stays behind the lock.

  • The screen at rest is wallpaper and a pill: unchanged. Widgets appear only with something to say; nothing is added at rest.

  • One chip per project with a dot per session; hover peeks; what's running? opens a card: kept exactly; the card is Sessions' full face and still opens on asking or a click, never on its own.
  • A notification is one line in the pill with a mark on the dot; the your-turn line and the Tab walk; the agent's question and provider trouble in the pill: kept; Needs you is the walk shown as rows when two or more sessions wait.
  • What are you watching? with stop buttons: kept as the card for standing watchers; Watching shows only what is counting now.
  • Welcome back and while you were away: kept; Away is the same group as a card with buttons, and leaves once seen.
  • The closing line is the receipt, with Undo and Details: unchanged; Now shows the ticked route and says Undo is above the pill. The red step's can't be undone now also appears while it runs, with Esc stops it.
  • The pill narrows to 360px with an app in front; a capsule for full-screen: unchanged; the desk's modes follow them, and the row is laid out as strips, pill, strips.
  • The stage, the shelf, cards in the column: unchanged; widgets never use the column.
  • What skips the model: extended with the word desk and with widget names in the launcher's name list, so show and hide reach them. Hover-peek and click-to-keep now apply to every strip.
  • A pin turns a card into an app: kept; keep this on the desk makes that app also a widget.

  • Rewind folds into Time; the Map's colours: unchanged; Away opens Time, the desk uses the Map's colours, and a corner of the Map lives on the desk only after show alive.

Reading note: this section is dated 27 Sep 2026, before the desk was built. On 2026-10-01 three of its statements about the code no longer hold: Codex's todo list reaches the plan event whole, the pill's row is laid out as strips, pill, strips, and the kit is on main. The vendor facts were not re-checked. Where the pieces stand.

  • The bar today is one Quickshell PanelWindow per screen on the Overlay layer with an input mask and exclusiveZone: 64 (shell.qml); a PanelWindow reserves its whole width by default when anchored to three edges, so the rails set exclusionMode: Normal and exclusiveZone: 0 explicitly.
  • PillState.qml is fed by agentd's JSON events, drops kinds it does not know, and guards status against another turn's id; agentd stamps every event with the current turn. DeskState follows the same pattern: plan carries the turn and is matched, desk events are not turn-bound.
  • Claude Code 2.1.283's stream has no TodoWrite: the plan arrives as TaskCreate and TaskUpdate, one task per call, which narrate.py already reads one at a time. Codex's todo list arrives per update with only done or not done per item, and providers.py today keeps only the first unfinished one; the plan event needs the whole list.
  • Apps open floating and centred in their own special workspace from a window rule (placement.py), sized to the monitor's usable area; with no exclusive zone on the rails, a 560×680 app on a 1280-wide screen never touches a 300px rail. Hyprland's dim_special is at its default in the shipped config, so what lies under a special workspace is already dimmed.
  • The pill row today is one centred Rectangle capped at 900px with the queue chips above it (shell.qml); the row as strips, pill, strips is new layout in the bar window.
  • The kit has Panel, ListRow, Meter, Ring, Sparkline, StackedBar, Badge and Stat (share/qml/Bombadil, on an unmerged branch when this was checked), so every face is composition, not new drawing, except the Map corner, which the Brain app owns.
