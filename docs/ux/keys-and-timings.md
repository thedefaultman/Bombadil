## Keys

The only bind file on `main` is `iso/airootfs/etc/skel/.config/hypr/hyprland.lua`, and it has no `hl.bind` other than the ones below. Two of them, the tap on `Super` and `Alt+Space`, are not commands: they name global shortcuts that the bar registers (`shell/PillKeys.qml`), and Hyprland drops one the bar has not registered without a word. It also sets `input = { kb_layout = "us", follow_mouse = 1 }` (line 43). Keys that reach the pill's field (`Enter`, `Esc`, `Tab`) work only while the pill holds the keyboard. On Hyprland that is while it is summoned (`WlrKeyboardFocus.OnDemand`, else `None`); on other compositors the layer is `Exclusive` while summoned and `OnDemand` otherwise (`shell/shell.qml:366-368`). Under a full-screen window the field is disabled (`enabled: !win.capsule`, `shell/shell.qml:759`), so nothing can be typed there.

### On the Widgets card

### A message or task card

Nothing on a card sends but its button, and a key never presses a button that is not lit.

### In a coding session's window

A coding session's window is `foot` (`footclient` while the foot server runs) on a zellij session. The repository sets no key for it. zellij starts with its default keybinds cleared (`keybinds clear-defaults=true`) and in `locked` mode, so none of zellij's own keys, the ones that switch mode included, does anything, and every key reaches Claude Code, Codex or the shell. The one key-related zellij setting is `support_kitty_keyboard_protocol true`, and the headless desktop test shows `Shift+Enter` reaching Claude Code through foot and zellij as a line break, not a send. The keys of the foot window itself (scrollback, copy, paste, font size) are foot's defaults, which nothing in the repository sets, so this page does not list them. Of the Hyprland binds above, the one that acts on the window is `Super+Q`, which closes it, and because zellij detaches when its window is force-closed (`on_force_close "detach"`) the session keeps running and its window comes back by the session's name (`share/zellij/config.kdl:5-7, 11, 14`, `src/bombadil/dev.py:428, 525-532`, `iso/airootfs/etc/skel/.config/hypr/hyprland.lua:94-102`, `tests/desktop/driver.py:2462-2471`).

## The launcher words

agentd answers these without a model. A typed line is lowercased, its whitespace is collapsed and the signs ` .!?,;:` are trimmed from both ends (`normalize`). Then only spaces, hyphens and underscores fold away (`_key`), so `wi-fi`, `wi fi` and `wifi` are one word. A line that has any other sign, or a letter outside `a-z`, matches no word in the tables. Five things get past that: an app's own title (so `Café` opens), a picture phrase and a widget question, which hold their own apostrophes, the unit name in `what does <unit> need`, and `what's running`. A line that starts with `!` is never a word: `match` returns nothing for it (`src/bombadil/launcher.py:372-373`), and agentd runs it as a shell command turn, with no model, signed in or not (`src/bombadil/agentd.py:2715-2717, 2793`). The list is exact on purpose: a parser that guessed would give the machine two brains that sometimes disagree. The code is `src/bombadil/launcher.py`, called from `AgentD._match` (`src/bombadil/agentd.py:844-850`).

`match` tries these in order, and the first that fits wins (`src/bombadil/launcher.py:367-445`):

1. A line that is empty or starts with `!`: not a word (371-376).
2. `why`, only while a turn runs (381-382).
3. A picture phrase, or `what does <unit> need`, unless an app has exactly that title (383-386).
4. A widget question, such as `how's the machine`, unless an app has exactly that title (387-389).
5. A sign-in word (390-391).
6. A provider verb and a provider name (392-397).
7. `pause` or `resume` and a provider name or `the ai` (398-403).
8. A core command (404-408).
9. A coding-session word: a project, a session, `claude|codex|shell <project> [role]`, `end ...`, or a phrase that lists the sessions. Tried only when agentd hands over its sessions (`dev.Dev`), and not when the text is one of Bombadil's own fixed words (a model word, a brain word, a Tips word, a Mail word or a panel word, with or without an open, close or hide verb: `_fixed_word`, 354-364) (409-412).
10. A model word (413-415).
11. A brain word (416-418).
12. A Tips word, the whole line and no other: `tips`, `the tips`, `open tips`, `show tips`, `open the tips`, `show the tips`, `what can you do`, `what can I ask` or `help` (419-421, the words at 76-84).
13. The words of messages and connections: `connect <service>`, and the words that open the unread messages or `Connected here`, bare, with `the` before them or after an open verb (`_connect_action`, 448-461) (422-424).
14. Each of the open, close and hide verbs, then no verb (425-444). For each, the Mail words come first, then an app, then a panel, then (open only) a utility command.
15. A widget with a verb: `open`, `show` or `bring up`, `close`, `hide` or `put away`, or `keep`. A line that ends in `?` skips this step (445). The `open` verbs skip `tips`, whose open words are step 12's (`APP_WIDGETS`, 168-172, 493).

Apps beat panels and panels beat utility words, so an app called `Sound` opens as itself. Mail is the exception, and so are the words of Tips and of messages and connections: they come before apps, so `apps.create` refuses an app called `tips` or `mail` (`apps.RESERVED`) and one called `slack`, `messages`, `unread`, `connections` or `connected` (`apps.CLAIMED`), and Tab never offers an app of such a name. A picture phrase and a widget question lose to an app with exactly that title. A widget name loses to an app with that name. Core commands beat apps. A project or a session name comes after the core commands, so it never shadows one. It beats an app word and a utility word of the same name (with a folder called `sound` or `battery`, those words and `open sound` or `open battery` name the project), but never a model word, a brain word, a Tips word, Mail's words or a panel's (the words of messages and connections are not among them: a folder called `slack`, `messages`, `unread` or `connections` takes that word): with a project folder called `mail`, `open mail` still opens Mail, and with one called `browser`, `browser` still opens the panel, while `claude mail` and `claude browser` name the project. It takes only the bare word and the open verbs, so `close passwords` and `hide passwords` still reach an app called Passwords even when a folder has that name. A utility word matches bare or after an open verb, so `the sound` alone goes to the agent while `the browser` opens the panel (`src/bombadil/launcher.py:441-444`).

What the pill itself offers is narrower, and only a hint: agentd decides. `launcher.entries` lists apps (Mail as its own entry, with its words), then the projects and sessions (each project folder, its three forms `claude myapp`, `codex myapp` and `shell myapp`, and each session the person named, by its role), then panels, then widgets, then the first word of each core, brain, Tips and utility command (so `Tips`, `Remote` and `Install` are offered), leaving out `restart`, `shutdown`, `lock`, `stop`, `volume_up`, `volume_down`, `mute`, `unmute` and `whyhere`, then `Sign in`, then `Messages` and `Connections` (`src/bombadil/launcher.py:175-176, 503-530`, `src/bombadil/dev.py:261-274`). agentd sends the list again when an app, a project folder or a session name appears or goes. `Tab` completes from that list once two characters follow an optional verb, and a widget's name completes only after `open`, `show`, `close` or `hide`, or, for a widget that has a row on the Widgets card (the entry says `keep`), after `keep`, with or without ` on the desk` after it. The grey `↵ Passwords` appears when the text is exactly one of those words (`shell/shell.qml:824-832`, `shell/PillState.qml:1006-1078`). A widget entry carries `open: false` when nothing answers `show <word>`, and the pill then hints and completes it only after the desk's own verbs; the Tips widget's entry never says so while the Tips words are in the table, because they answer `show tips` (`src/bombadil/launcher.py:516-523`, `shell/PillState.qml:1001-1004`). `PillState` knows eight of the launcher's verbs (`open`, `show`, `launch`, `start`, `close`, `quit`, `hide`, `keep`) and not `run`, `bring up`, `go to`, `switch to`, `exit`, `kill` or `put away`, so those open the app without the hint (`shell/PillState.qml:978-983, 985-1078`).

### Where the code does not yet meet a rule

Each is read from the file named.

- **Hyprland's own animations are not reached by reduced motion.** Under `BOMBADIL_REDUCE_MOTION=1` every animation in the bar, the desk and the kit has a still version: the three duration tokens read `0`, any other duration goes through `Theme.duration`, and a loop holds still or pulses (`share/qml/Bombadil/Theme.qml:93-101`, `share/qml/Bombadil/Stone.qml:44, 276-283`, `shell/DeskCard.qml:48-68`, `share/qml/Bombadil/Style/BusyIndicator.qml:53-60`), and `tests/test_motion.py:558` fails an animation that has none. What the switch does not reach is Hyprland's slide of windows, panels and app drawers and its fades (`iso/airootfs/etc/skel/.config/hypr/hyprland.lua:52-55`), and Qt's own scrolling inertia and cursor blink. Nothing in the repository sets the variable, so reduced motion is off until someone puts it in the session's environment ([MOTION.md](../MOTION.md)).
- **One number for one meaning.** A finished job is hidden by the shell after `12000` ms and dropped by the table after `15.0` s (`shell/DeskState.qml:118`, `src/bombadil/jobs.py:40`). The app chips' `150` ms border fade (`shell/shell.qml:644`) and a `Diagram` box's `140` ms move (`share/qml/Bombadil/Diagram.qml:400-402`) are literals that are neither `fast` nor `normal`.
- **Tokens for the desk's durations.** `foldMs`, `unfoldDelayMs` and `washMs` live in `shell/DeskTheme.js:51-53`, not in `Theme.qml`, the desk wraps them in `Kit.Theme.duration` where it uses them, and `tests/test_theme.py` does not compare them.
- **A hint that is narrower than the launcher.** Seven of the launcher's open, close and hide verbs show no `↵` hint (see [The launcher words](#the-launcher-words)).
